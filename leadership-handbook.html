Purpose

The operational guide for MBLI school and network leaders.

This handbook is the operational guide for school and network leaders at Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana. It establishes required protocols, decision-making standards, documentation expectations, and accountability structures that govern how leaders implement and enforce policy across both campuses. It supplements — and does not replace — the Employee Handbook.

Internal Use Only

This document is for internal leadership use only and is not to be distributed to general staff.

Introduction & Framework

The purpose, mission, and governing framework of this handbook.

Mission, Vision & Leadership Commitments

Organizational Vision

Every child is known, loved, and flourishing.

Organizational Mission

We personalize learning within a restorative community, fostering resilience and excellence while preparing students for real-world opportunities.

Leadership Mission

Matchbook leaders create the conditions for students and staff to succeed. We lead with clarity, integrity, and urgency — building systems, developing people, and making decisions grounded in evidence and equity.

Leadership Core Values

ValueWhat It Means
Student-CenteredEvery decision is grounded in its impact on student achievement, safety, and opportunity.
Equity Through LeadershipLeaders actively dismantle barriers by designing equitable systems and advocating for students and families.
Clarity & AccountabilityLeaders set clear expectations, communicate directly, follow through, and view accountability as an act of care.
Instructional PriorityTeaching, learning, and student mastery drive all improvement decisions.

Leadership Expectations

Acceptance of a leadership role constitutes acknowledgment of and agreement to the following expectations. Failure to meet them may result in corrective action, removal from a leadership role, or termination.

All leaders are expected to:

  • Prioritize student outcomes — academic achievement, safety, and equitable access
  • Provide effective instructional leadership, including data-informed decision-making and oversight of academic programs
  • Establish clear expectations, communicate directives professionally, and address performance concerns promptly
  • Comply with all organizational policies and applicable law, including student safety and reporting obligations
  • Supervise, evaluate, and support staff with proper documentation and corrective action per policy
  • Ensure operational and fiscal responsibility within approved budgets and compliance standards
  • Engage in ongoing professional development and implement feedback received through supervision
  • Collaborate with network leadership and fulfill assigned duties across individual school or department responsibilities

Organizational Structure & Roles

How the network is organized, and who is accountable for what.

Network Overview

Matchbook Learning operates as a unified K–12 network providing instructional coherence, operational consistency, and equitable outcomes across two campuses. Network leadership — the COO, K–8 Head of School, and High School Head of School — sets strategic direction, ensures system-wide alignment, and provides operational and instructional support for both schools.

Reporting structure:

Staff→ Immediate Supervisor→ Assistant Principal / Director→ Head of School→ COO / Executive Leadership

Leadership Role Summaries

Chief Operating Officer (COO)

The COO oversees all operational, financial, and organizational support systems. The COO holds final decision-making authority over operations, HR, finance, and compliance, and evaluates Heads of School. The COO signs off on all final employment actions (hire, promotion, non-renewal, termination).

Head of School — K–8 and High School

Each Head of School is the chief instructional and operational leader of their campus, accountable for student achievement, staff performance, school culture, and daily operations. Heads of School have authority over day-to-day operations within network policy parameters. Strategic, financial, and network-wide decisions require COO coordination.

Assistant Principals

Tier 1 AP (K–8): Focuses on core instruction, classroom practice, and student engagement. Makes day-to-day instructional and operational decisions within scope assigned by the Head of School.

Tier 3 AP (K–8): Leads intensive student support, behavior intervention, and specialized services. Decisions involving long-term placement, expulsion, or significant legal risk require Head of School and network coordination.

High School AP: Supports instruction, student culture, and daily operations. Decisions involving long-term discipline, graduation eligibility, or policy interpretation require Head of School coordination.

Directors & Specialized Leaders

Director of CTE: Leads design, implementation, and compliance of career and technical education programs. Budget and compliance decisions require HOS and network coordination.

Director of CBL: Leads the K–12 competency-based learning framework design, consistency, and professional learning. Campus implementation decisions are made collaboratively with school leaders.

Director of Workforce Development: Leads workforce-aligned programs and employer partnerships. Financial and contractual decisions require executive coordination.

Dean of Student Culture (K–8 & HS): Maintains safe, consistent school climate and leads behavior systems. Suspension or significant disciplinary actions require HOS coordination.

Instructional Coaches & Department/Grade-Level Leads

Instructional Coaches are non-evaluative partners in professional growth. They support teaching practice through coaching cycles aligned to school priorities. Personnel or evaluation decisions are made by school leadership, not coaches.

Department Chairs and Grade-Level Leads facilitate instructional coherence and collaborative practice within their teams. They hold no formal evaluative authority. Final decisions on curriculum, staffing, or policy remain with school leadership.

Shared Services & Coordinators

Operations & Data Integrity: Establishes and enforces district-wide operational procedures, data standards, and compliance processes. May audit, reconcile, and require corrective action when records do not meet standards. Reports to COO.

Enrollment & McKinney-Vento Liaison: Manages district-wide enrollment, recruitment, and outreach. Serves as official McKinney-Vento Liaison ensuring immediate enrollment and services for students experiencing homelessness. Reports to COO.

Operations Specialist: Supports the COO with HR compliance, onboarding, contracts, licensure tracking, personnel records, benefits/leave workflows, fiscal documentation, and vendor compliance. Reports directly to the COO.

Special Education Coordinators: Oversee IEP/504 implementation, compliance timelines, and documentation. Serve as the primary point of contact for special education audits and compliance.

03

Culture & Climate Leadership

Building and sustaining a safe, consistent staff and student culture.

Staff Culture

School leaders are accountable for the health of staff culture in their buildings. Leaders must model professionalism, set clear behavioral and performance expectations, address misalignment promptly, and foster an environment of psychological safety and equity.

Leaders are required to:

  • Address conduct concerns directly and privately; never allow gossip or passive resistance to persist
  • Reinforce expectations consistently through meetings, coaching, and evaluations
  • Balance high support with high accountability
  • Take corrective action — through performance management or disciplinary processes — when conduct undermines collaboration, trust, or student outcomes

Failure to model or enforce staff culture expectations will be addressed through coaching, evaluation, or corrective action.

Student Culture

Leaders must establish, model, and sustain a safe, inclusive, and academically focused student culture. Consistency, equity, and data use are non-negotiable.

Leaders are required to:

  • Define, teach, and reinforce schoolwide behavior expectations aligned to the Code of Conduct and Restorative Practices Standards
  • Protect instructional time — intervene promptly when behavior disrupts learning
  • Hold staff accountable for applying expectations and consequences consistently across all classrooms and settings
  • Monitor behavior, attendance, and climate data to identify trends, inequities, and improvement opportunities
  • Ensure families are informed, engaged, and supported as partners

Leaders have authority to:

  • Establish school-specific culture procedures within the district framework
  • Determine and apply disciplinary responses consistent with policy and law
  • Require restorative interventions, behavior plans, and re-entry meetings
  • Remove students from classrooms or activities when necessary to maintain safety and learning
  • Direct staff corrective action related to student culture implementation

Restorative Practices & Behavior Management

Restorative practices are embedded in daily culture — not reserved for incidents alone. Leaders must ensure they are applied consistently, equitably, and in alignment with the Student Culture Framework and Code of Conduct.

Important

Restorative practices may NEVER be used to delay necessary disciplinary action or compromise student or staff safety. When safety is at risk, disciplinary action must occur first.

Leaders must ensure restorative practices are:

  • Proactive — relationship-building structures exist before conflict occurs
  • Responsive — harm is addressed promptly with accountability and repair identified
  • Equitable — applied consistently; data is reviewed regularly for disproportionality
  • Documented — agreements include clear actions, timelines, and adult follow-up

Disciplinary action is required (regardless of restorative practices) when:

  • There is a threat to safety
  • Required by law or policy
  • Behavior is severe or repeated

Non-Classroom Settings: Hallways, Cafeteria & Arrival/Dismissal

Leaders are responsible for the safety, order, and efficiency of all non-classroom spaces. High-traffic transitions are a direct reflection of school culture and leadership presence.

Required leadership actions:

  • Establish clear routines and supervision coverage for all transitions
  • Maintain visible presence during arrival, dismissal, hallway transitions, and lunch
  • Assign, train, and hold staff accountable for supervision duties
  • Intervene directly when behavior disrupts safety or order
  • Ensure families receive clear communication regarding arrival/dismissal procedures

Crisis & Safety Leadership

Leaders are responsible for maintaining safe, secure school environments and responding to crises with calm, decisive action. Preparedness is not optional.

Leaders must:

  • Maintain up-to-date school safety and crisis response plans aligned to legal requirements
  • Ensure all staff are trained annually on emergency procedures
  • Conduct required drills (fire, lockdown, severe weather) and debrief outcomes
  • Take immediate action to protect life during any emergency — do not delay due to uncertainty
  • Coordinate with emergency services, district leadership, and agencies as required
  • Communicate timely, accurate, and calm information to staff, students, and families
  • Document all incidents accurately and complete all required reports within required timelines
  • Facilitate recovery plans including restorative and mental health supports after incidents
Important

Do not delay action when safety is at risk. Contact 911 first, then notify leadership.

Instructional Leadership

Leading instruction, coaching, and data-driven improvement.

Core Instructional Vision & CBL Framework

High-quality Tier 1 instruction is the foundation of student achievement at MBLI. All students deserve daily access to grade-level, rigorous, and culturally responsive instruction. Matchbook operates a Competency-Based Learning (CBL) model in which students progress based on demonstrated mastery — not time. CBL is the school’s core instructional operating system.

Leaders are required to:

  • Clearly articulate and reinforce MBLI’s instructional vision, including CBL expectations, curriculum fidelity, and culturally responsive practice
  • Ensure instruction is standards-aligned, organized into competency-level learning targets, and supports flexible pacing
  • Protect and monitor uninterrupted, bell-to-bell instructional time
  • Lead data cycles that analyze mastery gaps, growth trends, and instructional causes
  • Ensure proactive, tiered intervention aligned to competency data — and provide acceleration for students who master early
  • Hold staff accountable for planning, delivery, assessment practices, and CBL implementation

What leaders should see in classrooms (Tier 1 Look-Fors):

  • Clear learning targets and success criteria visible and referenced during instruction
  • High student engagement, academic discourse, and checks for understanding
  • Standards-aligned lessons using adopted instructional materials
  • Differentiation, scaffolds, and small-group instruction embedded within grade-level work
  • Multiple practice-feedback-revision cycles; student ownership of learning

Observations, Walkthroughs & Feedback

Frequent, focused observation and actionable feedback are the primary tools for improving instruction. Walkthroughs are non-evaluative by default unless explicitly designated as part of a formal evaluation process.

Observation requirements:

  • Leaders must conduct weekly walkthroughs across grade levels and content areas (3–10 minutes each; consistent, equitable coverage)
  • Formal observations meet Indiana law and the board-approved evaluation framework requirements
  • All observations are focused on instruction, student experience, and classroom culture
  • Every observation must result in timely, specific, and actionable feedback aligned to the instructional framework

Core instructional look-fors (all walkthroughs):

  • Planning & Purpose: Clear learning targets and success criteria
  • Instruction & Engagement: Active student engagement, appropriate cognitive demand, questioning, and discourse
  • Differentiation: Small-group instruction and responsive adjustments to student needs
  • Assessment & Feedback: Formative checks that advance learning
  • Learning Environment: Safe, respectful culture and efficient routines

Leaders must:

  • Maintain accurate, professional observation records and clearly distinguish coaching notes from formal evaluation records
  • Participate in calibration activities to ensure inter-rater reliability and reduce bias
  • Use observation trends to guide coaching priorities and professional development
  • Never use walkthroughs as surveillance, discipline tools, or public comparison of teachers

Teacher Coaching

Teacher coaching is a continuous, job-embedded leadership responsibility — not a compliance exercise. Every teacher benefits from coaching. Coaching is developmental by design, not corrective by default.

All instructional leaders (HOS, APs, Deans, Instructional Coaches) must:

  • Treat coaching as a protected, prioritized responsibility — allocate dedicated time and maintain classroom presence
  • Implement consistent coaching cycles: observe or review data → identify a high-leverage focus → provide actionable feedback → teacher practice → follow-up observation and reflection
  • Differentiate coaching: increase frequency for new teachers, new hires, and staff implementing new curricula; provide stretch coaching for high-performing staff
  • Ensure coaching conversations are evidence-based, include teacher reflection and clear next steps, and have a follow-up timeline
  • Maintain clear coaching documentation and distinguish it from formal evaluation records
  • Ensure coaching trends inform professional development planning
Important

Coaching may never be used as discipline. When concerns require formal action, follow HR and evaluation policies immediately.

Professional Development

PD at MBLI is purposeful, data-driven, and job-embedded. Leaders are accountable for ensuring adult learning translates into observable instructional change.

Leaders are required to:

  • Develop and maintain a PD plan aligned to student data, observation trends, and instructional priorities
  • Ensure all PD aligns to MBLI’s CBL framework, instructional frameworks, and adopted curriculum systems
  • Design PD grounded in authentic classroom practice: model, practice, collaborate, reflect — not passive lecture
  • Establish clear, observable instructional look-fors communicated to staff in advance
  • Conduct post-PD walkthroughs focused on look-fors and provide aligned coaching feedback
  • Evaluate PD effectiveness using student data, observation trends, and teacher feedback; refine plans continuously

Data-Driven Instruction

MBLI operates a data-driven instructional model in which student learning data informs daily instruction, intervention, acceleration, and improvement. Data is a tool for action, not compliance.

Leaders are required to:

  • Implement protected, weekly data meetings using a consistent protocol focused on: What did students learn? Who has mastered the standard? Who has not — and why? What will change instructionally?
  • Ensure NWEA MAP data is analyzed at classroom, grade, and school levels to monitor growth and inform intervention
  • Align daily instruction to state standards; use ILEARN results to identify priority standards and inform schoolwide planning — reactive test prep is prohibited
  • Ensure curricular tools are used with fidelity to monitor unit-level mastery and support teacher instructional decisions
  • Intentionally disaggregate data to identify opportunity gaps and ensure instructional responses address inequities
  • Ensure data directly drives flexible grouping, timely intervention, and acceleration for students demonstrating mastery
  • Document data decisions; monitor implementation of instructional adjustments; adjust when strategies are ineffective

Operational Leadership

Daily operations, attendance, scheduling, facilities, and budget.

Daily Operations

Strong daily operations are a core instructional leadership function, not a separate responsibility. Small operational failures compound into instructional loss.

Leaders are required to:

  • Ensure instruction starts and ends on time; transitions are efficient; schedules are honored
  • Maintain high visibility during arrival, dismissal, transitions, and common areas
  • Ensure classrooms are ready daily: materials functional, spaces clean and safe
  • Monitor staff attendance daily; ensure timely coverage for absences; prioritize instructional continuity and equity in all coverage decisions
  • Monitor student attendance and tardiness daily; respond quickly; process late arrivals efficiently
  • Communicate clearly and promptly; provide timely updates on operational changes
  • Ensure daily safety procedures and supervision requirements are met proactively
  • Collaborate closely with operations, office, custodial, and support service staff to resolve concerns quickly

Attendance Protocols

Student Attendance

Attendance is an instructional priority, not solely an operational or compliance function.

Leaders must:

  • Ensure attendance is taken accurately and reviewed daily by designated leadership
  • Implement a tiered, documented, and timely response: Tier 1 (family outreach for initial absences), Tier 2 (repeated absences — check-ins and barrier-focused support), Tier 3 (chronic absenteeism — coordination with support services and formal plans)
  • Address tardiness as an instructional issue; minimize disruption for late arrivals
  • Ensure students can re-engage academically after absences without academic penalty without support

Staff Attendance

Staff attendance directly impacts learning, classroom stability, and school culture. Operations maintains the official attendance system; school leaders ensure accurate daily reporting.

Leaders must:

  • Submit complete and timely attendance information to Operations daily (absences, late arrivals, early departures, flex/WFH days, and coverage needs)
  • Secure coverage quickly, prioritizing student safety, instructional continuity, and equity
  • Monitor attendance patterns; address concerns early through supportive, documented conversations
  • When excessive absences may involve medical conditions, disability, or other protected circumstances: immediately inform the employee that supports may be available and notify Operations/HR — do not delay referral to FMLA, ADA, or other applicable protections
Important

Operations must never be kept uninformed of reasons for excessive absences. Failure to notify HR/Operations of potential protected leave circumstances creates compliance risk.

Schedule Flexibility & Remote Work — 12-Month Staff

Schedule Flexibility (Flex Time)

Flex time is a scheduling tool — not leave, not PTO, and not an entitlement. Operational coverage and continuity take priority at all times.

Permitted with supervisor approval: Adjusted start/end times, short mid-day blocks (≤3 hours), or shifted hours within the same workweek.

Not permitted without written HOS/COO approval: Blocks exceeding 3 hours, recurring patterns reducing weekly presence, or any use during blackout periods.

Blackout periods — flex time is prohibited during:

  • First and last week of school
  • State testing windows
  • Audit, ADM, or compliance deadlines
  • Within 2 business days of school breaks or summer transitions

Process: Submit dates, times, reason, and coverage plan at least 1 business day in advance. Supervisor approves/denies and documents. HR/Operations maintains records and audits quarterly.

Remote Work

Remote work is a management tool, not a benefit or entitlement. On-site presence is the default for all school-based leadership roles.

Requirements for any approved remote work:

  • Must not compromise student safety, supervision, or family service
  • Must be intentional, documented, and approved in advance (at least 1 business day)
  • Partial-day remote follows Schedule Flexibility policy
  • Full-day remote requires supervisor approval; recurring patterns require COO approval
  • Staff must remain reachable, attend required meetings, and be prepared to return on-site for urgent needs

Same blackout periods as flex time apply. Non-exempt staff must record all hours; off-the-clock work is strictly prohibited. Failure to follow this policy may result in revocation of remote work privileges and corrective action.

Facilities Management

Safe, clean, well-maintained facilities are essential to instruction. Facilities management is a shared responsibility: School Leadership ensures daily instructional readiness and reports needs; Operations owns systems, compliance, execution, and long-term planning.

School leaders are required to:

  • Ensure classrooms and common spaces are ready for instruction daily
  • Monitor facilities conditions daily through walkthroughs — identify cleanliness, maintenance, safety, or functionality concerns
  • Report all facilities issues promptly via the Operations work order system; follow escalation protocols for urgent or safety-related concerns immediately
  • Reinforce daily cleanliness expectations; hold staff and students accountable for building standards
  • Ensure emergency exits, hallways, and access points remain unobstructed at all times
Important

Safety issues supersede all other facilities priorities. Operations must never be kept uninformed of issues affecting safety, compliance, or instruction.

Budget, Enrollment & Staffing Policy

Compliance with this policy is a condition of employment for all principals and school leaders. This policy is aligned with MBLI’s charter, applicable state law, and board-approved financial practices. The COO holds final oversight authority for implementation, enforcement, and interpretation. In any conflict with internal documents, board-approved policies, charter agreements, and applicable law take precedence.

The approved annual budget represents the maximum allowable expenditure for the school year. Enrollment changes do not automatically justify increased spending. All staffing, purchased services, and operational decisions must align with the approved financial plan.

Budget Compliance

All schools must operate strictly within the approved annual budget. Leaders are required to:

  • Approve expenditures only within allocated budget lines
  • Request all budget modifications through Operations in writing before spending occurs
  • Review monthly budget-to-actual reports; act on discrepancies immediately
  • Communicate anticipated overages to the COO as soon as they are identified — surprises are unacceptable
  • Use all organizational funds responsibly, ethically, and in alignment with mission and policy

Leaders may NOT:

  • Exceed budgeted amounts without prior written COO approval
  • Enter new vendor contracts without prior written approval
  • Expand purchased or outsourced services without approval
  • Bypass purchasing processes or commit funds based on assumed future revenue

Enrollment & Staffing Alignment

Staffing decisions must be directly tied to actual student enrollment and approved class size thresholds. Enrollment growth alone does not justify additional staffing. All four conditions below must be met before a new teacher or section may be added:

  • Enrollment consistently exceeds the defined class size threshold
  • The increase is sustained — not temporary or based on projections
  • Additional sections are required to maintain instructional quality
  • Prior written approval is obtained from the COO or designee

Class size guidelines (Indiana standards):

Purchased Services & Vendor Management

Principals are responsible for managing all contracted services within approved budget constraints. The following are required:

  • All purchased services must align with approved budget allocations — no exceptions
  • New vendor contracts require prior written COO approval before any commitment is made
  • Existing vendor agreements must be reviewed annually for cost efficiency and continued alignment with school needs
  • Expansion of outsourced services without prior approval is prohibited

Unauthorized vendor contracts or service expansions may be voided and may result in personal financial liability for the authorizing leader.

Financial Oversight Responsibilities

Principals must maintain active, ongoing financial oversight. Required actions include:

  • Monthly review of budget-to-actual performance for all cost categories
  • Continuous alignment of staffing levels with actual enrollment data
  • Early identification and immediate escalation of any financial risks to the COO
  • Full compliance with all financial reporting requirements and submission deadlines

Financial oversight is not a periodic or year-end function — it is an active, ongoing leadership responsibility. Leaders who fail to identify and report financial risks in a timely manner will be subject to corrective action regardless of whether a budget overrun ultimately occurs.

Teacher-to-Student Ratio Policy

MBLI calculates teacher-to-student ratios using total enrolled students divided by instructional Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) positions. This ratio measures the level of instructional support available to students and is not equivalent to average class size, which is governed separately by the Budget and Enrollment Staffing Policy.

Ratio Formula

Calculation: Total Enrolled Students ÷ Instructional Teacher FTEs — Example: 500 students ÷ 25 FTEs = 20:1 ratio. Part-time staff are included at their assigned FTE (e.g., 0.5 FTE for half-time).

Positions Included in FTE Count

The following positions are included when the employee’s primary responsibility is direct student instruction:

  • General Education Teachers (Elementary, Middle, High School)
  • Career and Technical Education (CTE) Teachers
  • Related Arts and Elective Teachers
  • Special Education Teachers
  • MLL/ELL Teachers
  • Academic Intervention, Reading Intervention, and Math Intervention Teachers
  • Instructional Specialists assigned to direct student instruction

Positions Excluded from FTE Count

  • School Administration: Head of School, Principal, Assistant Principal, Deans, Directors, and Executive Leadership Team
  • Instructional Coaches who do not provide direct student instruction
  • Student Support Services: Counselors, Social Workers, Psychologists, and Nurses
  • Paraprofessionals, Instructional Assistants, and Classroom Aides
  • Operations, Administrative, Technology, Facilities, Food Service, and Transportation Staff

Reporting

The standard teacher-to-student ratio — using instructional FTEs only — is MBLI’s official staffing ratio. For operational planning, an expanded ratio that includes intervention and specialist staff providing direct student services may also be reported; such ratios must be clearly labeled to distinguish them from the official figure.

Ratios are reviewed annually as part of the budget and staffing planning process. The COO and Executive Leadership Team hold final authority to interpret and implement this policy.

Human Resources & Compliance

Evaluations, discipline, documentation, and HR partnership.

HR Structure

All HR and compliance functions operate through a single network-wide HR department that supports both campuses equally. HR reports directly to the COO — not to principals.

Principals support HR processes by:

  • Ensuring staff submit required forms and comply with HR deadlines
  • Providing performance feedback to inform HR processes
  • Implementing HR-determined corrective action steps
  • Communicating network HR policies to their campus

Principals do NOT:

  • Supervise Operations/HR staff or staff from other campuses
  • Issue threats or discipline to HR/Operations staff
  • Redesign HR roles or duties
  • Block access to staff files, logs, or compliance information
  • Make unilateral HR decisions
  • Conduct HR investigations without COO/HR involvement

This separation protects the network from claims of bias, retaliation, or inconsistent treatment.

Required Campus Compliance

  • Submit PTO/sick logs to HR daily
  • Submit licensure documentation at the start of each semester or upon request
  • Submit onboarding files within required deadlines
  • Submit staff evaluations within required deadlines
  • Follow network HR procedures uniformly — do not alter, delay, or withhold HR documentation

Staff Evaluations

All certificated staff must be evaluated in compliance with Indiana Code 20-28-11.5, IDOE requirements, and MBLI’s board-approved evaluation framework. Consistent, timely evaluations support instructional improvement and inform employment decisions.

Annual requirements for all certificated staff:

  • Non-probationary teachers: at least one (1) formal observation per year plus additional informal observations per framework
  • Probationary teachers: observations meeting or exceeding framework requirements; additional as required by licensure or performance
  • All observations include timely, documented feedback
  • Summative rating finalized in the approved system, acknowledged by the staff member, and submitted to Network HR
Important

All summative evaluations must be completed and submitted to Network HR no later than August 1 following the end of the school year. No alternate timelines are permitted. Failure to meet this deadline is a supervisory performance issue.

Exceptions

Exceptions are not automatic and must be documented and approved by Network HR. Qualifying circumstances include:

  • Late hire (after January 1): Evaluator completes as much evidence as reasonably possible; summative may defer to following year
  • Leave of absence >50% of the instructional year: Summative may defer; protected leave status does not negatively affect employment standing

Progressive Discipline

Progressive discipline is corrective, not punitive. Similar issues will be handled consistently across roles and campuses. HR involvement is required when issues have legal, contractual, or policy implications.

Discipline steps (MBLI reserves the right to skip steps based on severity):

  1. Informal coaching and feedback
  2. Documented verbal warning
  3. Written warning
  4. Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
  5. Suspension or administrative leave
  6. Termination or non-renewal

School leaders are responsible for:

  • Clearly communicating performance expectations and standards
  • Addressing concerns promptly; providing and documenting informal coaching
  • Monitoring patterns of behavior or performance over time
  • Maintaining professionalism and confidentiality throughout

Leaders MUST consult HR before issuing:

  • Written warnings
  • Performance Improvement Plans
  • Suspensions or administrative leave
  • Recommendations for termination or non-renewal

Leaders may NOT:

  • Issue written discipline without HR review and approval
  • Promise or imply specific disciplinary outcomes
  • Conduct independent investigations involving misconduct, discrimination, harassment, or retaliation
  • Request or collect medical documentation directly from employees

Immediately notify HR when:

  • Performance concerns may lead to written discipline
  • Attendance issues may involve medical, ADA, or FMLA considerations
  • An employee raises concerns about discrimination, harassment, or retaliation
  • Allegations involve student safety, misconduct, or legal risk
  • Suspension, termination, or non-renewal is being considered
Important

Failure to involve HR in these circumstances creates compliance risk and may invalidate disciplinary action.

Documentation Standards for Discipline

All disciplinary documentation must be:

  • Objective and fact-based — free of emotional or subjective language
  • Clearly tied to policy, expectations, or performance standards
  • Reviewed and approved by HR prior to issuance for any written discipline

When communicating discipline: Be clear, respectful, and direct. Focus on behavior — not character. Avoid guarantees, threats, or speculation. Communicate expectations, timelines, and next steps clearly.

Disciplinary decisions must never be based on medical conditions, protected leave, pregnancy, or any protected characteristic. HR must review any disciplinary action involving employees with known or potential protected status.

Personnel Documentation & Disciplinary Records

All employee performance and disciplinary documentation must be consistent, accurate, legally compliant, and maintained in the official personnel record. This policy applies to all supervisors, including Heads of School, administrators, and managers.

Official Personnel File

All formal disciplinary and performance-related documentation must be submitted to HR and maintained in the employee’s official personnel file. Documentation not submitted to HR may not be treated as part of the official record and may not be used to support employment decisions.

Only documentation in the official personnel file may be used to support discipline, termination, promotion, or compensation decisions.

No Shadow Files

Supervisors may not maintain separate or unofficial personnel files containing disciplinary or performance records. Temporary working notes may be used for meeting preparation or routine observations, but any information used for discipline or corrective action must be formally documented and submitted to HR.

Prohibited storage locations for personnel and disciplinary records:

  • Personal email accounts or inboxes
  • Personal devices or drives
  • Unsecured shared folders or local hard drives
  • Informal notes kept separate from the official HR file

Required HR Review Before Issuance

The following documentation must be submitted to and reviewed by HR before or at the time it is issued to the employee:

Document TypeHR Review Required
Documented verbal warningYes — before or at time of issuance
Written warningYes — before or at time of issuance
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)Yes — before or at time of issuance
Suspension or administrative leaveYes — before or at time of issuance
Final written warningYes — before or at time of issuance
Recommendation for terminationYes — COO final approval required

HR reviews all disciplinary documentation for consistency, fairness, legal compliance, and policy alignment before it becomes part of the official record.

Confidentiality & Employee Access

Personnel records must be stored securely in MBLI-approved systems and treated as strictly confidential at all times. Supervisors must not store disciplinary records in personal files, email folders, or unsecured locations.

Employees will receive copies of all formal disciplinary documentation issued to them and may access their personnel records as permitted by law and MBLI policy. Requests for personnel file access must be directed to HR.

Important

HR monitors compliance with this policy and provides guidance to supervisors. Failure to follow this policy may invalidate disciplinary action and will be treated as a leadership performance issue.

Communication & Documentation Standards

Communication and documentation are core leadership responsibilities. Leaders must assume all written and documented records may later be reviewed by HR, executive leadership, families, authorizers, regulators, legal counsel, or courts.

All leadership communication and documentation must be:

  • Professional — respectful, composed, appropriate in tone
  • Clear and factual — accurate, concise, neutral, and unambiguous
  • Timely — completed as soon as practicable after an event or concern
  • Complete — sufficient to stand alone if reviewed later
  • Confidential — shared only with individuals with a legitimate need to know

Leaders may NOT:

  • Use sarcasm, profanity, or emotionally charged language in written communication
  • Communicate disciplinary decisions without HR involvement
  • Use informal channels (text/personal apps) for sensitive or high-stakes matters
  • Share confidential information without authorization

When to notify HR and/or executive leadership immediately:

  • Threats of legal action or media contact from families
  • Allegations of staff misconduct
  • Safety concerns or serious incidents
  • Potential legal claims, investigations, or law enforcement involvement

Required Documentation — When and What

Leaders must document same day or within 24 hours:

  • Student incidents (behavioral, disciplinary, safety, medical)
  • Staff conduct or performance concerns
  • Parent/guardian complaints that escalate beyond the classroom
  • Safety, compliance, or risk-related events

Documentation must include: Date/time/location, names and roles, objective description of what occurred, immediate actions taken, notifications made, and next steps.

Documentation Language Standard

Appropriate: “Student raised voice and stated, ‘I’m not doing this,’ then left the classroom.”

Inappropriate: “Student was defiant and disrespectful.”

All documentation must be stored in approved MBLI systems. Personal notes, personal devices, or private email accounts may not serve as the sole record. Documentation is confidential and may only be accessed by individuals with a legitimate educational or operational need.

07

Student Services & Support Systems

MTSS, special education, MLL services, and family engagement.

Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)

MBLI implements MTSS to address academic, behavioral, attendance, and social-emotional needs across all students. MTSS applies across general education, special education, and student services.

Leaders must ensure:

  • Tier 1 core instruction is monitored, supported, and high-quality
  • Tier 2 and Tier 3 interventions are clearly defined, data-driven, and progress-monitored
  • MTSS teams meet regularly and document decisions
Important

MTSS may NOT be used to delay evaluation for special education or to deny legally required services.

Special Education (IEP & Section 504)

Special education compliance is a school-wide responsibility — not solely the responsibility of special education staff.

Leaders must ensure:

  • IEPs are implemented as written; services and minutes are delivered consistently
  • General education teachers understand and follow IEP accommodations
  • IEP meetings occur on required timelines; documentation is accurate, timely, and complete
  • 504 plans are implemented consistently; staff understand accommodations; reviews occur on required timelines
Important

Failure to implement an IEP is a compliance violation. While service models may differ between K–8 and 9–12, compliance standards do not.

Multilingual Learner (MLL) Services

Leaders are responsible for ensuring MLL services meet all state and federal requirements.

Leaders must ensure:

  • Accurate identification and placement of MLL students per IDOE guidance
  • Delivery of appropriate language support services integrated into core instruction
  • Monitoring of student progress toward English proficiency
  • Teachers understand MLL accommodations and instructional strategies
  • MLL services are not substituted with special education or intervention services unless legally appropriate
  • Required documentation is maintained and parent communication occurs in accessible languages

Counseling, Social-Emotional & Partner Services

Counseling and social-emotional supports promote student well-being, safety, and readiness to learn. Counselors and social workers are not disciplinarians and may not be used as a substitute for classroom management or administrative action.

Leaders must ensure:

  • Counseling services are aligned to student needs and campus priorities
  • Referral processes are clear and consistently applied
  • Crisis response protocols are followed
  • Partner agencies are approved in coordination with network leadership and Operations
  • Data sharing with partner agencies complies with FERPA; parent consent is obtained when required
  • Partner services do not operate independently of school systems or supervision

Student services decisions must be documented, with data guiding placement, interventions, and exits. Records are maintained in approved systems with confidentiality preserved at all times.

Family & Community Engagement

Family engagement is a leadership responsibility, not a delegated task. Leaders are accountable for the tone, accuracy, and consistency of all family communication.

Leaders must ensure:

  • Regular, proactive communication with families through clear, accessible channels
  • Timely follow-up on all family inquiries and concerns
  • Families receive clear communication on academic expectations, attendance, behavior, student services, safety, and key events
  • Communication is accessible to families with different language needs
  • Significant family communications are documented in approved systems

Escalate to network leadership or HR when concerns involve:

  • Allegations of staff misconduct
  • Safety issues, legal threats, or media contact
  • Requests for policy exceptions
  • Repeated or unresolved complaints

Community partnerships must be approved in coordination with network leadership, aligned to school priorities, and monitored for impact. Partners may not operate independently of school leadership or policies. FERPA and safety requirements apply to all partners.

08

Data, Accountability & Reporting

Data integrity and meeting compliance deadlines.

Data Collection, Systems & Integrity

Data integrity is a leadership responsibility. Accurate and timely data is essential for instructional decisions, compliance, and accountability to families, authorizers, and regulators.

PowerSchool is the official system of record for student enrollment, demographics, attendance, schedules, grades, and state reporting. All official student data must be accurately reflected in PowerSchool. Internal trackers are supplemental only — they must align with PowerSchool and may not replace required entries.

Leaders are required to:

  • Ensure data is entered accurately and on time by staff in the correct system
  • Monitor data quality; correct errors promptly and transparently
  • Review reports before submission; certify data to the best of their knowledge
  • Ensure staff use approved systems, accounts, and devices; protect confidential data per FERPA
  • Reconcile internal trackers with systems of record regularly; resolve discrepancies in the system of record
Important

Data may not be retroactively altered to improve outcomes or appearance. Back-dating or retroactive manipulation of records is strictly prohibited. Intentional falsification may result in termination and referral to appropriate authorities.

Compliance Deadlines

Meeting compliance deadlines is a non-negotiable leadership responsibility. Deadlines are external obligations, not internal targets.

This applies to all required submissions including:

  • State and federal reporting (IDOE, DOE, Title programs)
  • Authorizer-required submissions
  • Special education, 504, MLL, and MTSS timelines
  • Student attendance, enrollment, and ADM deadlines
  • Staff evaluations, licensure, and HR compliance
  • Financial, audit, and grant reporting

Leaders must:

  • Know applicable compliance deadlines for their campus
  • Assign clear ownership for required tasks and monitor progress
  • Manage work backward from the MBLI master Compliance Calendar
  • Immediately escalate to network leadership when a deadline may be missed, required information is unavailable, or errors are identified after submission

Early escalation is required and expected. Do not wait until a deadline has passed to report a risk. Missed deadlines may result in compliance findings, funding implications, or corrective action. Repeated failures will be treated as a leadership performance issue.

Leader Onboarding

Leader onboarding is a structured process — not informal shadowing. All new leaders will complete:

  • Review of the Employee Handbook and Leadership Handbook
  • Orientation to leadership routines, cadence, and key systems
  • Training on data platforms, compliance requirements, and evaluation frameworks
  • Clear performance expectations and 90-day goals

Chain of Command

The chain of command is a leadership expectation, not a courtesy. Issues must be resolved at the lowest appropriate level, escalated promptly when required, and communicated clearly back through the chain.

Standard chain (school-based):

Staff→ Immediate Supervisor (Dean / Dept. Head / Instructional Lead)→ Assistant Principal / Director→ Head of School→ Network Leadership (COO / HR / Executive)

Escalate when:

  • The issue cannot be resolved at the current level
  • Safety, legal, or compliance risks are present
  • Policy interpretation or exceptions are required
  • Cross-campus or cross-department coordination is needed
  • Timelines or deadlines are at risk
Important

Bypassing the chain of command is prohibited, except when: there is an immediate safety concern, mandatory reporting obligations apply, or HR/legal matters require direct notification. When bypassing occurs, the appropriate supervisor must be notified as soon as practicable.

Leaders may not seek alternative answers after a decision has been made, undermine another leader’s authority, or use informal channels to override decisions.

Decision-Making Protocols

Decisions must be clear, documented when appropriate, and respected once made.

Decision TypeWho DecidesExpectation
Leader-DecidesLeader, within their authorityDecide, document if needed, communicate clearly
Consult-Then-DecideLeader retains authority, seeks input firstGather input → decide → communicate rationale

Documentation is required when decisions: affect staffing, discipline, or evaluations; change policy or procedures; involve compliance, safety, or legal considerations; or commit resources or funds.

Decisions may be revisited only when: new material information emerges, implementation reveals unintended consequences, or directed by executive leadership. Disagreement alone is not grounds for reopening a decision.
