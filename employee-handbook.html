A Word About This Handbook

This Employee Handbook contains information about the employment policies and practices of Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana (MBLI). We expect employees to read this handbook carefully, as it is a valuable reference for understanding your job and MBLI. The policies outlined should be regarded as management guidelines only, which in a developing business will require changes from time to time. This iteration of the Employee Handbook supersedes and replaces any and all previous versions and any inconsistent verbal or written policy statements.

At-Will Employment Statement

Nothing in this Employee Handbook or in any document or statement, written or oral, shall limit the right to terminate employment at-will. No officer, employee, or representative of MBLI is authorized to enter into an agreement — express or implied — with any employee for employment for a specified period of time unless such an agreement is in a written contract signed by the Chief Operating Officer of MBLI.

Non-Contractual Disclaimer

This Employee Handbook is provided for informational purposes only. Nothing in this Handbook is intended to create, nor should it be construed as creating, a contract or agreement of employment or any contractual obligations between Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana Inc. and any employee. This Handbook does not guarantee employment for any specific period of time and does not create any fixed terms or conditions of employment, whether express or implied.

Employee Handbook Online Disclaimer

This website version of the Employee Handbook is intended to reflect the policies contained in the official Employee Handbook maintained by Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana. It is provided for employee convenience and general reference only.

In the event of any discrepancy, conflict, or ambiguity between this website version and the official Employee Handbook, the official Employee Handbook shall control. Employees should refer to the official document, available here, for the authoritative statement of company policy.

This website version does not create any contractual rights or obligations, and MBLI reserves the right to update, modify, or correct this version at any time without prior notice to align it with the official handbook.

Right to Modify Policies

MBLI reserves the right, in its sole discretion and subject to applicable law, to alter, eliminate, amend, or otherwise change any policy, procedure, or guideline described in this Employee Handbook at any time, with or without notice. The only exception is the at-will employment policy, which may be modified only by a written agreement signed by the employee and an authorized representative of Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana Inc. (including the School Leader or a designated representative).

Equal Opportunity, Ethics & Conduct

The standards that keep our community respectful and fair.

Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) Policy & Human Rights Act

MBLI complies with all applicable federal and Indiana employment laws including Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA), USERRA, and the Indiana Civil Rights Law (IC 22-9-1).

We will not discriminate against employees or applicants for employment on any legally-recognized basis or protected class including, but not limited to, veteran status, uniform service member status, race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, sexual orientation, genetic information, physical or mental disability, or any other protected class under federal, state, or local law. This policy governs all aspects of employment at MBLI, including hiring, assignments, training, promotions, compensation, benefits, discipline, discharge, and all other terms and conditions of employment.

You may discuss equal employment opportunity related questions with your Supervisor, Human Resources, or your School Leader.

General Harassment, Discrimination & Professional Bias

Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana (MBLI) is committed to maintaining a professional work environment grounded in respect, equity, and accountability. Consistent with MBLI’s Professional Conduct Policy, all employees are expected to interact with colleagues, students, families, and stakeholders in a manner that upholds dignity, professionalism, and mutual respect.

Violations of this policy constitute violations of the Professional Conduct Policy and may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

Harassment and Discrimination

MBLI prohibits harassment of or discrimination against any employee based on race, religion, color, national origin, physical or mental disability, marital status, military or veteran status, age, sex, sexual orientation, personal appearance, political affiliation, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law.

Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to: epithets, slurs, derogatory comments or jokes, intimidation, negative stereotyping, threats, physical interference, or any conduct that interferes with an employee’s work or creates a hostile, intimidating, or offensive work environment. Harassment or discrimination may occur in any form, including verbal, written, visual, or electronic communications such as email, text messages, online platforms, images, gestures, or postings related to the workplace.

Professional Bias

In alignment with MBLI’s expectations for professional conduct, the organization also prohibits professional bias — when an employee’s role, discipline, licensure type, degree, experience, or career pathway is unfairly elevated or diminished in ways that undermine trust, collaboration, or respect. Examples include:

  • Devaluing colleagues based on subject area, role, or credential
  • Questioning a colleague’s legitimacy or competence due to licensure type or career path
  • Implying that certain disciplines, roles, or leadership tracks are less important to student success or organizational effectiveness

All instructional, operational, and leadership roles are essential to MBLI’s mission. Professional disagreement is expected to focus on ideas, evidence, student outcomes, data, and instructional effectiveness — not personal qualifications or perceived status.

Sexual Harassment & Unwelcome Sexual Conduct

MBLI is committed to providing a workplace free of sexual harassment and other unwelcome sexual conduct — conduct that creates an offensive or hostile working environment, or that is made a condition of working at MBLI. Sexual harassment may occur between people of the same or opposite sex. Examples of prohibited conduct include:

  • Verbal harassment (e.g., sexual requests, comments, jokes, slurs)
  • Physical harassment (e.g., unwanted physical contact)
  • Visual harassment (e.g., posters, cartoons, emails, or drawings of a sexual nature)

Our workplace is not limited to MBLI’s facilities but includes anywhere a business or school-related function is taking place, including all MBLI-sponsored events. Such behavior may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Scope: This policy applies to all MBLI employees — including certified, classified, administrative, and leadership staff — and governs all professional interactions conducted in person or through written, digital, or electronic means. If an employee believes they have been subjected to conduct that violates this policy, they should report the concern promptly in accordance with the reporting procedures outlined below.

How to Report

An employee who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate these standards should immediately report the incident to Human Resources or their School Leader/supervisor. Reports may be made in person, in writing, or by telephone.

MBLI maintains an Open Door policy. Employees are never required to confront the individual involved before making a report. All reports are reviewed promptly and handled as confidentially as possible.

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith or participates in an investigation is strictly prohibited, and will be addressed as a serious violation of professional conduct.

Standards of Conduct

Employees are expected to conduct themselves in a mature and responsible manner. The following are examples of conduct that may result in disciplinary action, including suspension or termination. This list is illustrative only:

  • Conduct that diminishes confidence in your ability to perform your duties or compromises MBLI’s reputation
  • Theft or unauthorized removal or possession of property belonging to another
  • Falsification of timekeeping or other records
  • Working under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs
  • Fighting or threatening violence in the workplace
  • Boisterous or disruptive activity in the workplace
  • Negligence or improper conduct leading to damage of MBLI property
  • Insubordination or other disrespectful conduct
  • Violation of safety or health rules
  • Sexual or other unlawful harassment or discrimination
  • Possession of dangerous or unauthorized materials in the workplace
  • Excessive absenteeism or any absence without notice
  • Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information
  • Inappropriate activities involving children
  • Providing false or misleading information
  • Failure to cooperate in or interfering with an MBLI investigation

Nothing in this policy is intended to interfere with employees’ rights under the National Labor Relations Act to engage in protected concerted activity.

Conflicts of Interest Policy

MBLI is committed to maintaining the highest standards of integrity and transparency in all of its operations.

Acceptance of Gifts

Before accepting any gift from a vendor, individual, or organization with whom you or MBLI has a past, current, or potential future relationship, you must first consult with MBLI’s General Counsel or Chief Operations Officer (COO). This policy applies regardless of the value of the proposed gift or the employee’s specific role.

Service on External Boards

Any employee invited to serve on the board of directors or advisory board of another organization must disclose this in writing to both their Head of School and the COO before accepting. MBLI reserves the right to request that an employee decline or resign from such a position if it is incompatible with the employee’s duties or MBLI’s mission.

Reporting & Enforcement

Failure to disclose a potential or actual conflict of interest may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination. Employees are encouraged to seek guidance from the General Counsel or COO if unsure whether a situation may constitute a conflict.

Whistleblower Policy & Procedures

MBLI is committed to lawful and ethical behavior and requires the board, advisors, employees, and volunteers to act in accordance with all applicable laws, regulations, and policies. The objectives of the Whistleblower Policy are to prevent, detect, and correct improper activities; encourage good-faith reporting of material violations of law or policy; ensure documentation and resolution of reports; and protect individuals who report concerns in good faith, in accordance with Indiana Code § 22-5-3-3, from retaliation.

Reporting Concerns

Employees: Whenever possible, resolve concerns by reporting directly to your Supervisor or the next level of management. If those channels cannot be used, contact the Chief Operating Officer. Reports should be in writing whenever practical.

Board Members, Advisors & Volunteers: May submit concerns directly to the chair of the Board. MBLI will investigate all reports with due care and promptness.

The types of concerns that should be reported include:

  • Providing false or misleading information on MBLI’s financial documents, grant reports, tax returns, or other public documents
  • Withholding material information from MBLI’s auditors, accountants, lawyers, directors, or other representatives
  • Embezzlement, private benefit, or misappropriation of funds
  • Material violation of MBLI policy, including confidentiality, conflict of interest, ethics, and document retention
  • Discrimination based on race, gender, sexual orientation, ethnicity, and disability

No director, advisor, employee, or volunteer who in good faith reports a concern shall be threatened, discriminated against, or subject to retaliation. MBLI treats all communications under this policy confidentially, except as necessary to conduct a complete and fair investigation.

Religious Accommodations

MBLI is committed to providing reasonable accommodations, where appropriate, for employees’ religious beliefs. It is the employee’s responsibility to notify their School Leader or Supervisor of the need for an accommodation. Accommodations should not interfere with or disrupt the normal routine of our organization.

In addition to these categories, employees are classified as exempt or non-exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

Hourly (non-exempt) employees are paid based on hours worked, must accurately record all time, and are eligible for overtime as required by law.

Salaried (exempt) employees are paid on a salary basis, are not eligible for overtime, and are expected to fulfill their responsibilities regardless of variations in work hours.

Classification is determined by job duties and business needs, not job title, and may be reviewed or adjusted to remain compliant with the law.

Immigration Reform & Control Act

MBLI hires and employs only individuals authorized to work in the United States. In compliance with the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA), all newly hired employees must complete Form I-9 as part of onboarding. Section 1 must be completed no later than the first day of work for pay, and the School will review original documentation and complete Section 2 through an E-Verify system.

Employees with time-limited work authorization are responsible for maintaining valid authorization throughout employment and must present updated documentation before expiration. All Form I-9 records are maintained securely and accessed only by authorized staff.

Employment Authorization & Visa Sponsorship

Matchbook Learning hires individuals who are legally authorized to work in the United States and who can maintain that authorization throughout employment.

As a matter of company policy, Matchbook Learning does not sponsor employment-based visas or work authorization applications (including H-1B, TN, E-3, O-1, or other petitions), and does not sponsor applications for permanent residency (“green card” sponsorship).

Applicants and employees are responsible for maintaining valid work authorization throughout their employment. Employment offers and continued employment are contingent upon the employee’s ability to provide and maintain documentation demonstrating authorization to work in the United States, as required by applicable law.

Nothing in this policy alters Matchbook Learning’s obligations under federal, state, or local law. Matchbook Learning reserves the right to modify or make exceptions to this policy at its sole discretion, consistent with applicable legal requirements and business needs.

Internal Transfer Policy

MBLI supports internal transfers between Matchbook Learning K–8 and The Match High School & Career Center to promote staff growth while maintaining instructional continuity and operational stability.

Eligibility & Approval

Employees seeking a transfer must hold required certifications or endorsements, demonstrate relevant experience and proficiency, and have a positive performance history.

A written transfer request goes to the Supervisor, with copies to the Operations Specialist and COO, including rationale, timeline, and updated credentials. An internal interview may be required. All transfers require approval from both the sending and receiving Head of School; if consensus is not reached, the COO makes the final decision.

Timing, Status & Benefits

Transfers are typically approved between academic years; mid-year transfers are generally not permitted except in urgent circumstances with COO approval.

Employees retain employment with MBLI. Salary placement may change based on role requirements, and core benefits continue though eligibility may vary if classification changes.

Compensation & Pay Practices

How and when you are paid, and related pay policies.

Pay Periods & Payroll Schedule

Most MBLI employees are paid semi-monthly on the 15th and the last day of each month. Salaried employees are generally paid over a 12-month period. Hourly employees are generally paid during the 10-month academic year based on hours worked and approved timekeeping, and are not paid during school breaks unless scheduled (except for designated paid holidays). When a payday falls on a Saturday or Sunday, employees are normally paid the following Monday; when a payday falls on a holiday, they are normally paid the next business day.

Direct Deposit or Bank-Drafted Paycheck

All full-time and part-time employees may receive pay via direct deposit or bank-drafted payroll check. Employees who elect direct deposit may view pay stubs online through Amplify Passport at https://www.amplifyhr.com/login. Direct deposit is MBLI’s preferred method.

Bank-drafted checks are mailed to the employee’s home address on file via courier and typically arrive within 2–4 business days after payday; employees are responsible for keeping their mailing address current in Amplify.

Reimbursements & Awards

All reimbursement requests must be approved by the employee’s School Leader or Supervisor, then submitted with receipts to the COO for review. Once approved by the COO, the reimbursable amount is added to the employee’s next regular paycheck.

MBLI may also recognize positive performance through monetary awards, generally issued through the next scheduled paycheck and subject to applicable payroll taxes.

Stipend Agreement Policy

MBLI may offer stipend payments for approved work outside of an employee’s regular job responsibilities, such as additional duties, extended time commitments, special projects, or supplemental assignments. All stipends must be approved in advance and documented through a written stipend agreement outlining the scope of work, duration, expectations, and payment terms.

All approved stipend payments will be paid through the employee’s regular payroll in accordance with the payment timeline and conditions specified in the stipend agreement. Stipend payments will be administered in compliance with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and do not alter an employee’s base salary or exemption status.

Payroll Corrections & Overpayments

Employees are responsible for reviewing each paycheck for accuracy and must report suspected errors to HR or Payroll as soon as possible.

Payroll Corrections

If an employee is underpaid, MBLI corrects the error promptly after verification, via an off-cycle payment or the next regular paycheck.

Overpayments

If an employee is overpaid, MBLI notifies the employee and explains repayment options; deductions comply with Indiana Code 22-2-6-2 and require written authorization. Repayment will not reduce pay below minimum wage, and reasonable repayment plans may be arranged.

Protections and Compliance

Employees will not be retaliated against for reporting payroll errors. All payroll corrections will be documented and maintained in accordance with recordkeeping requirements.

Timekeeping

Non-exempt (hourly) employees are required to accurately record all time worked each day. The employee’s immediate supervisor or designee will record the employee’s time in and out using the timekeeping method provided by Operations. Timesheets must be submitted according to established deadlines and approved by the employee’s manager. Non-exempt employees must also record all applicable leave time using the appropriate codes.

Important

No employee has authority to direct another employee to underreport time worked, omit leave taken, or submit an inaccurate timesheet. If anyone instructs you to report time inaccurately, notify your manager, Human Resources, and/or the COO immediately.

Overtime

All overtime for non-exempt employees must be approved in advance and in writing by the Head of School or the Chief Operating Officer. Supervisors do not have independent authority to approve overtime; requests must be submitted to and approved by the Head of School or COO before the overtime is worked.

Pre-approved overtime days include: First Semester Parents in Touch Day, Winter Celebration, Second Semester Parents in Touch Day, and the End of Year Student Showcase.

MBLI’s workweek runs Sunday 12:01 a.m. through Saturday 11:59 p.m. Non-exempt employees are paid overtime at 1.5× their regular rate for all hours worked over 40 in a workweek, in accordance with federal and state law. This applies to all hours actually worked, whether or not the overtime was approved in advance.

Employees who work unapproved overtime will still be paid for those hours, but working overtime without prior written approval from the Head of School or COO is a violation of this policy and will result in progressive discipline, up to and including termination.

Supervisor Timekeeping and Tracking Responsibility

Because supervisors (or their designee) are responsible for entering and maintaining hourly employees’ time on the timesheet, they are also responsible for monitoring those hours proactively rather than discovering overtime after the fact. Specifically, supervisors must:

  • Review timeclock data for each hourly employee under their supervision on a weekly basis, at minimum, to identify employees approaching or exceeding 40 hours before the workweek ends.
  • Flag any employee trending toward overtime to the Head of School or COO as early in the week as possible, so a decision on approval (or a schedule adjustment to avoid it) can be made before the threshold is crossed.
  • Document any instance of unapproved overtime discovered during timesheet entry, including when and how it was identified, for HR and payroll records.

Repeated failure by a supervisor to catch or report unapproved overtime before payroll processing will be addressed as a performance concern for that supervisor, separate from any discipline issued to the employee.

Pay Deductions

MBLI is required by federal and state law to make certain deductions each pay period, including federal income tax, state and local income tax, and Social Security and Medicare taxes (FICA). Additional deductions may include benefit premiums, retirement contributions, court-ordered garnishments, or other legally authorized withholdings.

Exempt (salaried) employees’ pay will not be reduced in a manner that violates salary basis requirements; salaries may be reduced only in the following circumstances:

  • Absences of one or more full days for personal reasons other than sickness or disability
  • Absences of one or more full days due to sickness or disability, provided a bona fide sick leave or disability plan exists and the employee has exhausted all available leave under that plan
  • Unpaid leave taken under the FMLA
  • Full-day suspensions for violations of safety rules of major significance
  • Full-day disciplinary suspensions for violations of written workplace conduct policies
  • Proportional salary during the first and last weeks of employment based on days actually worked
  • Reduction for negative leave balances, applied only in full-day increments

Garnishment & Child Support

When wages are garnished by court order, MBLI is legally bound to withhold the amount indicated, honoring federal and applicable state guidelines that protect a certain amount of income from garnishment.

Staff Attendance & Punctuality

Attendance and punctuality are essential to employee success at MBLI and to maintaining consistent support for students. Employees should refer to their campus-specific addenda for detailed attendance procedures, including staff arrival and dismissal times, daily reporting expectations, and campus-based coverage protocols.

If you will be late or absent, you must notify your School Leader or Supervisor before the start of the workday. Lateness is defined as arriving after your scheduled start time, and any unapproved late arrival will be charged against the employee’s available PTO in the nearest quarter-hour increment. Repeated tardiness, unapproved absences, or failure to follow call-in procedures may result in corrective action, up to and including termination.

Voluntary Abandonment

If an employee is absent for three (3) consecutive workdays without notifying MBLI, MBLI will consider the position voluntarily abandoned and the employee may be removed from payroll. This provision does not apply when the absence may qualify for protected leave under federal or state law.

Personal Time Off (PTO) Policy

MBLI provides Paid Time Off (PTO) to support employee well-being while maintaining strong instructional continuity for students. This policy establishes a clear, simple, and consistent approach to leave that allows flexibility for illness, personal needs, and family responsibilities.

MBLI’s academic calendar includes scheduled breaks that provide extended opportunities for rest and personal time, including Fall Break, Week of Thanksgiving, Winter Break, Spring Break, and Summer Break. These breaks are an important component of the overall time-off structure.

Annual PTO Allotment

At the start of each school year, eligible full-time employees receive 10 days (80 hours) of PTO. PTO is provided as a single, flexible bank and may be used for both personal and medical needs. PTO is front-loaded at the beginning of the school year. Employees hired after the start of the school year receive a prorated allotment based on their hire date.

Partial-Day Absences (Salaried-Exempt Employees)

A salaried-exempt employee who works only part of a scheduled workday must use available PTO, in quarter-hour increments, for the time not worked. Employees may not choose unpaid time to preserve their PTO. Using PTO does not reduce the employee’s regular salary.

If the employee has no PTO available, the absence must still be reported and approved. MBLI will address the time under the appropriate attendance, leave, performance, scheduling, or accommodation procedures without making an improper salary deduction. This rule does not prevent salary deductions otherwise permitted by law, including certain full-day absences, unpaid FMLA leave, or time not worked during the first or final week of employment.

FMLA and Pregnancy-Related Exceptions

If a partial-day absence qualifies as intermittent or reduced-schedule FMLA leave, a salaried-exempt employee may request that the qualifying time be unpaid rather than using PTO. Any salary deduction will be limited to the FMLA-designated time, and MBLI may require or allow PTO to run concurrently with FMLA leave.

An approved pregnancy-related accommodation under the PWFA or ADA does not automatically make a partial-day absence unpaid. Unless the absence qualifies for FMLA leave or another legal exception, the employee must follow MBLI’s standard PTO requirements. Pregnancy, childbirth, and related conditions will be treated consistently with other medical conditions when determining whether paid or unpaid leave is available.

Documentation

PTO is recorded in quarter-hour (¼-hour) increments.

MBLI may require reasonable documentation supporting an absence when:

  • An employee is absent for three (3) or more consecutive workdays;
  • A pattern, frequency, or timing of absences raises concern;
  • Documentation is necessary to determine whether an absence qualifies under this policy or another applicable leave policy; or
  • The circumstances surrounding an absence reasonably require verification.

Depending on the reason for the absence, acceptable documentation may include medical certification, a police or incident report number, court or government records, documentation confirming a qualifying bereavement or family emergency, vehicle repair, towing, or insurance records, or other reliable documentation reasonably related to the stated reason for the absence.

MBLI will limit documentation requests to information reasonably necessary to verify the absence and administer its leave policies. Employees must submit requested documentation within 5 business days or within the timeframe specified by MBLI.

Failure to provide requested documentation may result in the absence being denied, designated as unapproved or unpaid, or addressed under MBLI’s attendance and disciplinary policies, subject to applicable law. Providing false, altered, or misleading documentation may also result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

All medical and other confidential documentation will be maintained separately from the employee’s regular personnel file and handled in accordance with applicable law and MBLI’s confidentiality practices.

Fitness for Duty

Following an extended PTO absence due to illness or injury, MBLI may require an employee to provide a fitness-for-duty certification before returning to work, particularly when reasonable concerns exist regarding the employee’s ability to safely and effectively perform the essential functions of their role. This certification must come from the employee’s treating healthcare provider and confirm the employee is medically able to return to work, with or without reasonable accommodation.

Employees may be placed on leave (paid or unpaid, consistent with applicable policy) pending receipt of a satisfactory fitness-for-duty certification. MBLI will engage in the interactive process to evaluate any need for reasonable accommodation in accordance with the ADA and applicable state law. Failure to provide a requested certification within a reasonable timeframe, or falsification of such certification, may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

No Carryover and No Payout

Unused PTO does not carry over from one school year to the next. All unused PTO is forfeited at the end of the school year and is not paid out under any circumstances, including end of school year, resignation, termination, or retirement.

Misuse of Leave

Misuse or falsification of PTO may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Attendance Incentive Bonus (Annual)

MBLI recognizes the critical importance of consistent staff attendance in maintaining high-quality instruction and a stable learning environment for students. This policy establishes an annual incentive to reward exemplary attendance.

Eligibility

The Attendance Incentive Bonus is available to all full-time, student-facing employees who are not administrators. Student-facing roles include, but are not limited to, Classroom Teachers, Special Education Teachers, Instructional Assistants (IAs), Front Desk Personnel, and any staff member whose primary responsibilities involve direct interaction with and support of students.

Employees whose start date falls after the first day of the school year are not eligible for the attendance bonus for that school year. Employees must be actively employed at the time of payout to receive the bonus.

Eligible employees will receive the attendance bonus for maintaining perfect attendance throughout the full school year, defined as no use of personal time off (PTO) during the school year.

The following absences do not affect eligibility:

  • Days out due to ADA accommodations
  • Jury duty
  • Military leave
  • Bereavement leave (as defined by policy)
  • School-approved professional development days
  • School closures or cancellations

Eligible employees receive a $1,500 annual bonus, paid on the July 30 payroll following completion of the prior school year.

Unpaid Leave & Absenteeism

Unpaid Leave

Consistent with applicable law, employees may be granted unpaid leave in appropriate circumstances; contact your School Leader, Supervisor, or HR Manager for more information.

Excessive Absenteeism

Unapproved or excessive absenteeism may negatively impact an employee’s record, evaluations, and contract status. Family & Medical Leave Act (FMLA)

MBLI complies with the FMLA, providing eligible employees with job-protected unpaid leave for qualifying family and medical reasons. Employees are eligible if employed by MBLI for at least 12 months (not necessarily consecutive), having worked at least 1,250 hours in the preceding 12 months, at a worksite where MBLI employs 50 or more employees within a 75-mile radius.

Eligible employees may take up to 12 workweeks of unpaid FMLA leave during a rolling 12-month period, and up to 26 workweeks of Military Caregiver Leave in a single 12-month period.

Qualifying reasons include:

  • The employee’s own serious health condition
  • Birth and bonding within 12 months
  • Placement for adoption or foster care and bonding within 12 months
  • Care for a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition
  • A qualifying exigency related to covered military service
  • Military Caregiver Leave

Notice and Request Procedures: foreseeable leave requires at least 30 days’ advance notice when practicable; unforeseeable leave requires notice as soon as practicable.

Medical Certification & Benefits Continuation: Medical certification must be submitted within 15 calendar days of request. MBLI maintains group health coverage during FMLA leave under the same terms, with employees responsible for their premium contributions.

Return to Work & Reinstatement: Upon timely return from FMLA leave, employees will be reinstated to the same or an equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and terms of employment. MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee for requesting or taking FMLA leave.

Attendance Blackout Periods

To ensure uninterrupted instruction and continuity of operations, planned PTO or personal leave may not be taken during designated blackout periods. The Blackout Calendar is published at the beginning of each academic year and includes:

  • The first five instructional days of each school year
  • One school day before and after Fall, Thanksgiving, Winter, and Spring Breaks
  • One (1) school day before and after 3 day weekends
  • State or district testing windows
  • Parent–teacher conference days/evenings
  • Mandatory professional development or all-staff days
  • Major school events designated by the Head of School
  • The last five instructional days of the school year

Absences not approved or legally protected during blackout periods are unpaid and coded as an Unpaid Day Off; medical documentation is required for any absence due to illness during a blackout period to be coded as paid leave.

This policy does not limit rights under FMLA, ADA, military leave, or other legally protected leave; bereavement, jury duty, and subpoenaed witness leave are governed by their respective policies.

Accommodation for Disabilities (ADA)

MBLI is committed to providing equal employment opportunities to qualified individuals with disabilities in accordance with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and applicable state and federal laws. MBLI also complies with the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and other applicable medical leave laws.

Employees who believe they need a workplace accommodation may request one verbally or in writing by notifying their School Leader, Supervisor, or Human Resources. Employees will be asked to complete an ADA Accommodation Request Form, which initiates a good-faith interactive process to identify reasonable accommodations.

Reasonable accommodations will be provided unless doing so would impose an undue hardship or significantly disrupt school operations. MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee for requesting or using a reasonable accommodation.

Pregnancy, Childbirth & Related Conditions

MBLI is committed to providing a workplace free from discrimination and to supporting employees during pregnancy, childbirth, and related medical conditions. In accordance with the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) and the Indiana Civil Rights Law (IC 22-9-5), the organization will provide reasonable accommodations unless doing so would create an undue hardship.

Reasonable accommodations may include, but are not limited to:

  • More frequent or longer breaks
  • Temporary modification of work duties or schedules
  • Access to seating or the ability to sit rather than stand
  • Temporary relief from heavy lifting or strenuous tasks
  • Time off for prenatal medical appointments
  • Temporary reassignment to alternative duties when appropriate
  • Leave related to pregnancy or childbirth when necessary

The organization strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee who requests pregnancy-related accommodation, reports discrimination, or participates in an investigation related to these protections.

Background Checks & Safe Hiring

MBLI is committed to maintaining a safe and secure learning environment for students and staff. In accordance with Indiana law, federal law, and applicable charter authorization requirements, MBLI requires background checks for individuals who work with or have access to students.

The following individuals must successfully complete a background check prior to beginning service with MBLI: all employees (full-time, part-time, hourly, salaried), interns and student teachers, volunteers, contractors and consultants, and any other individual who has direct contact with students.

Background checks are conducted through MBLI’s designated vendor and include state and national criminal history checks, sex offender registry checks, and any additional screenings required by the Indiana Department of Education (IDOE), IPS Innovation Network, or other authorizing bodies. Individuals may not begin work or volunteer service until required background checks are completed and reviewed.

Sexual Abuse Prevention and Reporting Policy

MBLI is committed to providing a safe and respectful environment for all students and strictly prohibits sexual abuse in any form. MBLI maintains a zero-tolerance policy for sexual abuse by any employee, volunteer, contractor, board member, or third party. Confirmed violations will result in disciplinary action up to and including termination and may result in criminal prosecution.

Mandatory Reporting: All MBLI employees, volunteers, and contractors are mandatory reporters under Indiana law (IC 31-33). Any individual who has reason to believe a child has been abused or neglected must immediately report the concern directly to the Indiana Department of Child Services (DCS) and/or local law enforcement. Reporting to a supervisor or School Leader does not replace this legal obligation. Leadership may not delay, screen, or discourage reporting.

Anti-Retaliation: MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who, in good faith, reports suspected abuse or participates in an investigation. Retaliation is grounds for disciplinary action.

Alcohol, Drug & Tobacco Free Workplace

MBLI is committed to maintaining a drug, alcohol, and tobacco free environment. The following is prohibited on MBLI’s property (buildings, grounds, parking lots, garages, and fields) and at off-site activities with staff or students:

  • Possession, transfer, sale, distribution, use, or solicitation of illegal drugs
  • Possession or use of alcohol (unless specifically authorized by the School Leader)
  • Possession or use of tobacco products
  • Reporting to work intoxicated or impaired
  • Reporting to work with a detectable amount of any illegal drug
  • Abuse of prescribed drugs

MBLI maintains a policy of non-discrimination and reasonable accommodation with respect to recovering addicts and alcoholics. Violations may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

  • Fire drills (typically monthly)

If an evacuation is required, employees must follow directions from their Supervisor, School Leader, or other authorized MBLI personnel. Leave the building quickly and orderly, do not delay to collect personal items, and do not re-enter the building until cleared to do so.

MBLI prohibits the possession, carrying, use, display, or sale of weapons on MBLI property, in MBLI vehicles, at MBLI-sponsored events, or while conducting MBLI business on or off campus. “Weapons” include firearms, ammunition, knives (other than small pocketknives used for lawful purposes), tasers, explosives, and any object used or intended to cause harm.

This applies to all employees, volunteers, contractors, visitors, and others on school premises, including individuals legally permitted to carry a firearm. Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, removal from campus, and referral to law enforcement.

COBRA & Indiana Continuation of Benefits

MBLI complies with COBRA and applicable Indiana state continuation laws, which allow eligible employees and dependents to temporarily continue group health coverage at their own expense when coverage would otherwise end due to a qualifying event (such as termination other than for gross misconduct, reduction in hours, death of the covered employee, divorce or legal separation, or a dependent child losing eligibility).

Coverage duration is determined by law and may extend up to 18, 29, or 36 months; individuals electing continuation coverage are responsible for 100% of the premium plus any permitted administrative fee.

Upon a qualifying event, MBLI or its administrator provides written notice explaining options, deadlines, and payment requirements.

Certification, Licensure & Highly Qualified (HQ) Status

MBLI requires all instructional and student-facing staff to hold, maintain, and remain in good standing with the appropriate state certification, licensure, or Highly Qualified (HQ) status required for their position, in accordance with Indiana Department of Education (IDOE) requirements. Employees are responsible for ensuring their licenses remain valid, active, and in good standing with the State of Indiana at all times.

Employees hired without the required credential must complete an MBLI Education Plan for Certification or HQ Status, which outlines required steps, documentation, and timelines. Completion of the education plan is a condition of continued employment in the role.

Out-of-State Licensure Requirements

Employees hired with an out-of-state teaching license are required to:

  • Apply for an Indiana teaching license immediately upon hire
  • Apply for an Indiana Emergency or Temporary Teaching Permit, as applicable, to allow the employee to administer state-mandated assessments while the Indiana license application is pending
  • Obtain an Indiana teaching license by the start of the following academic year in which they were hired, unless an extension is permitted by IDOE and approved by MBLI

Non-Compliance

Failure to maintain required licensure, comply with an approved education plan, or obtain required credentials within established timelines may result in corrective action, reassignment (if available), non-renewal of contract, or termination of employment. Each case will be reviewed individually based on documented progress and circumstances.

Professional Development & Licensure Tracking

MBLI requires all certified staff to maintain compliance with IDOE professional development and licensure renewal requirements. This applies to all instructional and student-facing employees who hold or are required to hold an Indiana teaching license, administrator license, school services license, or other IDOE-issued credential.

Employee Responsibilities
  • Complete required Professional Growth Points (PGPs) within the licensure cycle
  • Ensure PD activities meet IDOE approval criteria
  • Track and upload PGPs in the IDOE licensing system
  • Monitor license expiration dates and renewal deadlines
  • Provide PD verification to MBLI upon request
MBLI Responsibilities
  • Communicate licensure and PD expectations upon hire and annually
  • Provide or facilitate access to IDOE-aligned PD when feasible
  • Monitor licensure status and PD compliance for audit and accreditation
  • Notify employees of identified deficiencies when possible

Eligible PD may include IDOE-approved trainings, conferences, school-provided PD, applicable coursework, coaching, PLCs, and compliance trainings that qualify under IDOE guidelines. Employees must retain PD documentation for the licensure cycle; MBLI may audit records and require corrective plans if compliance gaps are identified.

CPR / AED Certification

All certified staff and any teachers or other staff whose roles require IDOE licensure/certification or an IDOE-issued permit are required to maintain current CPR and AED certification as a condition of assignment.

  • Certification must be current and include CPR and AED (adult and child/school-age appropriate)
  • Training must be completed through an approved provider (e.g., American Red Cross, American Heart Association, National Safety Council, or comparable recognized provider)
  • Employees must keep certification valid and in good standing at all times and renew before expiration

Failure to obtain or maintain required CPR/AED certification may result in corrective action and/or temporary restriction from student supervision duties until compliance is restored, up to and including non-renewal or termination.

Corporate Credit Card Policy

MBLI corporate credit cards are issued for approved business expenses only and may not be used for personal purchases, cash advances, or cash-equivalent transactions. All corporate credit card purchases must be requested and processed through the Chief Operations Officer (COO). Staff may not independently make purchases on an MBLI corporate card unless specifically authorized by the COO for that transaction.

All charges must be supported by an itemized receipt and submitted to the COO (or designee) within ten (10) business days of the purchase for reconciliation and audit purposes. Charges without required documentation may be treated as unauthorized.

Misuse of the corporate card or failure to follow this process may result in loss of purchasing privileges, corrective action (up to and including termination), and reimbursement to MBLI. Lost or stolen corporate credit cards must be reported immediately to the COO and the employee’s School Leader.

Solicitation & Distribution Policy

To maintain a productive work environment, employees may not engage in solicitation during work time or distribute literature in work areas during work time. Employees may not solicit students, families, or visitors for personal business ventures, commercial products, or outside organizations while performing their job duties or during instructional time.

Limited Exception for Youth Fundraisers

MBLI recognizes that employees may wish to support their children’s participation in youth organizations and school activities. Employees may share information or accept orders for youth-related fundraising activities for their children (e.g., Girl Scout cookies, Boy Scout popcorn, youth sports team fundraising items) provided that:

  • Sales occur only during non-working time (before/after work, breaks, or lunch periods)
  • Employees may not pressure or require coworkers to participate
  • Sales may not interfere with school operations or instructional time
  • Fundraising materials may only be shared in staff areas such as break rooms or staff lounges, and may not be distributed to students or families without prior administrative approval

Employee Interactions with Students

To protect employees, students, and MBLI from allegations, rumors, or lawsuits, all MBLI faculty and staff will:

  • Not allow any student(s) or alumni to spend the night at his/her house or apartment
  • Not spend time outside of regular school hours with student(s) or alumni unless approved by the School Leader
  • Not engage in any activities that would endanger or corrupt any of our student(s) or alumni
  • Always behave to protect the safety, interests, and rights of all of our students and alumni
  • Report any misconduct that endangers the safety, interests, and rights of our students and alumni
  • Not engage in any inappropriate acts or behaviors with any MBLI student(s) or alumni
  • Notify the School Leader upon noticing, suspecting, or witnessing any inappropriate behavior between students and staff members, faculty, parents, volunteers, or other MBLI-affiliated personnel

Staff-Provided Student Transportation

MBLI does not require any employee to transport a student in a personal vehicle as a condition of employment, unless the employee’s job description expressly identifies student transportation as an essential job duty. On the limited occasions where a staff member volunteers and the School approves it — for example, a short-notice ride home or transport to a school-sponsored event — the arrangement is permitted only as a pre-approved exception under this policy and Form TR-B (Blanket Parental Transportation Authorization). No employee may transport a student until every requirement below is met and documented.

Positions Where Transportation Is a Job Duty

A small number of positions may expressly identify transportation of students in a personal vehicle as an essential job duty in the position’s job description. For an employee in such a position, providing this transportation is a condition of employment in that role, and maintaining Authorized Driver eligibility under this policy is a condition of continued employment in that position. If an employee in such a position becomes ineligible to serve as an Authorized Driver, including for a medical reason, MBLI will consider reasonable accommodations consistent with the ADA — which may include reassignment to a vacant position — before taking any adverse employment action.

Eligibility & Pre-Approval

To be approved as an Authorized Driver, an employee must:

  • Be a current MBLI employee in good standing — not on a Performance Improvement Plan and not subject to active disciplinary action.
  • Be at least 21 years old and have held a valid driver’s license for a minimum of three (3) consecutive years.
  • Hold a valid, unrestricted Indiana driver’s license (or valid equivalent for an out-of-state resident) that permits unsupervised operation of a passenger vehicle.
  • Consent to a motor vehicle record (MVR) check and a criminal history / child protection index check, both of which the School may review before granting approval and re-check annually thereafter.
  • Carry personal automobile liability insurance of at least $300,000 combined single limit (or equivalent split limits), with current proof of coverage on file with the School.
  • Drive a vehicle that is currently registered, in safe mechanical condition, and equipped with a functioning seatbelt at every seating position that will be used, and all signal lights functioning properly.
  • Complete and sign the Authorized Driver Acknowledgment on the Transportation Detail & School Verification form (Attachment A to Form TR-B) for each student to be transported.

Approval is granted in writing by the designated campus Transportation Director, Executive Director of the school or the Executive Director of Operations and Finance only, and only after every item above is verified. An employee may not transport a student based on a verbal go-ahead, an informal request from a parent, or their own judgment that the trip is minor or low-risk.

If the School becomes aware of a known medical condition that could reasonably affect an employee’s ability to safely operate a vehicle the employee may be asked to provide Operations with written clearance from a licensed healthcare provider confirming they are safe to transport students before Authorized Driver status is granted. This request will be individualized, job-related, and consistent with the ADA; it is not a standing requirement triggered merely by having a health condition on file, and the School will not inquire into a condition unrelated to safe driving. Any medical information obtained under this policy is kept confidential, maintained separately from the employee’s personnel file, and shared only with those who need it to administer this policy.

Disqualifying Conditions

An employee may not be approved — or, if already approved, must be immediately suspended from providing this transportation — if any of the following apply:

  • A suspended, revoked, or restricted license that would prevent unsupervised operation of a vehicle.
  • A DUI/OWI conviction within the preceding five (5) years, or more than one such conviction at any time.
  • Two or more moving violations within the preceding three (3) years.
  • A reckless driving, hit-and-run, or vehicular manslaughter conviction, at any time.
  • Lapsed, cancelled, or insufficient automobile insurance coverage.
  • An incomplete or expired MVR or background check.
  • Placement on a Performance Improvement Plan, or any active disciplinary action — particularly any matter involving student safety, supervision, or misconduct.
  • A vehicle that no longer meets the safety requirements above (expired registration, missing or non-functioning seatbelts, or an unsafe mechanical condition).
  • A medical condition that could reasonably impair the ability to safely operate a vehicle unless the employee provides current written clearance from a licensed healthcare provider confirming they can safely drive, with or without reasonable accommodation.

These conditions apply regardless of how the transportation arrangement came about, and regardless of whether a parent has already agreed to it. An employee who becomes aware that they no longer meet a requirement above must stop providing the transportation and notify the campus Transportation Director or the Executive Director of the school — before the next scheduled trip, not after.

Medical Reinstatement of Driving Privileges

An employee whose Authorized Driver status is suspended for a medical reason may request reinstatement at any time by providing updated written clearance from a licensed healthcare provider confirming the employee can safely operate a vehicle transporting students, including any conditions the provider recommends. The School will evaluate the request on an individualized basis, consistent with the ADA’s direct-threat standard, and may request a follow-up conversation with the employee’s healthcare provider with the employee’s written consent. Any medical information obtained under this policy is kept confidential, maintained separately from the employee’s personnel file, and shared only with those who need it to administer this policy. Reinstatement is not automatic upon submission of clearance and remains subject to final approval by the Executive Director of the school or Executive Director of Operations and Finance.

Ongoing Responsibilities

Once approved, an Authorized Driver must continue to:

  • Maintain a valid license, current registration, and qualifying insurance at all times.
  • Report any of the following to their supervisor within 24 hours: a moving violation, an at-fault or not-at-fault accident, a license suspension or restriction, a lapse in insurance coverage, a new DUI/OWI charge, or medical diagnosis that may impair their ability to safely transport students.
  • Require every occupant to wear a seatbelt for the entire trip.
  • Transport only students for whom a signed Form TR-B is on file, and travel only between the locations authorized on that student’s Attachment A.
  • Make no unauthorized stop, detour, or additional passenger.
  • Never use a mobile device while driving.

Failure to report a disqualifying event, or providing false or incomplete information at any point in this process, is treated as a serious integrity violation and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination — separate from, and in addition to, the loss of Authorized Driver status.

Revocation of Authorization

The Executive Director of the school or Executive Director of Operations and Finance may suspend or revoke an employee’s Authorized Driver status at any time, with or without cause, and with or without advance notice, whenever continuing the arrangement raises a safety, insurance, or liability concern. Revocation is not a disciplinary action in itself and does not require a PIP or formal write-up to take effect — it simply ends the employee’s eligibility to provide this specific transportation.

Related Forms & Documentation

This policy operates together with Form TR-B (Blanket Parental Transportation Authorization) and its Attachment A (Transportation Detail & School Verification). No student may be transported by a staff member until both (a) a signed Form TR-B is on file for that student authorizing Mode A — Staff-Provided Transportation, and (b) the employee’s Authorized Driver’s file is complete and current under this policy. Records are retained in the student’s file and Operations will file and maintain the Authorized Driver’s form and documents in the driver’s personnel file for the period required by School records policy, and in no event fewer than two years.

Children in the Workplace Policy

Purpose

Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana is committed to providing a safe, secure, and productive work environment for employees, students, and visitors. While the School recognizes that employees may occasionally experience unexpected childcare challenges, the workplace is not intended to serve as a substitute for regular childcare.

This policy establishes expectations regarding the presence of employees’ children and other minors in the workplace to protect student safety, maintain an effective learning environment, and reduce operational and liability risks.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, volunteers, interns, and temporary staff working at any Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana facility.

Policy

Employees are expected to arrange appropriate childcare during their scheduled work hours. Employees may not routinely or regularly bring their children or other minors to work in lieu of childcare.

Children may only be present on school property during an employee’s workday under one of the following circumstances:

  • The child is enrolled as a student at the school.
  • The child is attending a school-sponsored activity or event.
  • The child is making a brief visit that has been approved in advance by a member of the Executive Leadership Team.
  • An unexpected emergency has occurred and prior approval has been obtained from a member of the Executive Leadership Team.

Approval of any exception is at the sole discretion of the School and may be denied based on operational, safety, security, confidentiality, or supervisory concerns.

Employee Responsibilities

If approval is granted:

  • The employee remains solely responsible for the supervision, care, conduct, and safety of the child at all times.
  • The child may not disrupt instruction, meetings, student services, or normal business operations.
  • The child may not accompany the employee into confidential meetings, student disciplinary matters, counseling sessions, or areas where confidential student or personnel information may be discussed or viewed.
  • The child may not enter restricted or hazardous work areas unless participating in an approved school-sponsored activity.
  • School employees are not responsible for supervising another employee’s child.
  • If an employee is unable to perform the essential functions of their job because they must supervise a child, the employee may be required to use available leave or leave the workplace.

Administrative Authority

A member of the Executive Leadership Team may require a child to leave the workplace at any time if the child’s presence:

  • Creates a safety or security concern;
  • Disrupts school operations;
  • Interferes with employee productivity;
  • Impacts students or staff; or
  • Creates an unreasonable liability risk for the School.

Repeated requests for exceptions or repeated use of this accommodation may be denied and may result in corrective action if the employee fails to maintain appropriate childcare arrangements.

No Expectation of Childcare

Nothing in this policy creates an expectation that Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana will provide childcare or supervise employees’ children while employees are working.

Exceptions

Only members of the Executive Leadership Team may approve exceptions to this policy. Exceptions may be granted for Board-approved events, school-sponsored programs, “Take Your Child to Work Day,” or other unique circumstances determined by the Executive Leadership Team to be in the best interest of the School.

All substitute assignments must comply with IDOE licensure requirements, contracted vendor requirements, and MBLI policies. Improper assignment or misuse of substitute personnel may result in corrective action.
