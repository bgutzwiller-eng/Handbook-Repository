Introduction & Employment Relationship

Who we are, and the nature of your employment with MBLI.

A Word About This Handbook

This Employee Handbook contains information about the employment policies and practices of Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana (MBLI). We expect employees to read this handbook carefully, as it is a valuable reference for understanding your job and MBLI. The policies outlined should be regarded as management guidelines only, which in a developing business will require changes from time to time. This iteration of the Employee Handbook supersedes and replaces any and all previous versions and any inconsistent verbal or written policy statements.

At-Will Employment Statement

Nothing in this Employee Handbook or in any document or statement, written or oral, shall limit the right to terminate employment at-will. No officer, employee, or representative of MBLI is authorized to enter into an agreement — express or implied — with any employee for employment for a specified period of time unless such an agreement is in a written contract signed by the Chief Operating Officer of MBLI.

Non-Contractual Disclaimer

This Employee Handbook is provided for informational purposes only. Nothing in this Handbook is intended to create, nor should it be construed as creating, a contract or agreement of employment or any contractual obligations between Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana Inc. and any employee. This Handbook does not guarantee employment for any specific period of time and does not create any fixed terms or conditions of employment, whether express or implied.

Right to Modify Policies

MBLI reserves the right, in its sole discretion and subject to applicable law, to alter, eliminate, amend, or otherwise change any policy, procedure, or guideline described in this Employee Handbook at any time, with or without notice. The only exception is the at-will employment policy, which may be modified only by a written agreement signed by the employee and an authorized representative of Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana Inc. (including the School Leader or a designated representative).

Equal Opportunity, Ethics & Conduct

The standards that keep our community respectful and fair.

Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) Policy & Human Rights Act

MBLI complies with all applicable federal and Indiana employment laws including Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA), USERRA, and the Indiana Civil Rights Law (IC 22-9-1).

We will not discriminate against employees or applicants for employment on any legally-recognized basis or protected class including, but not limited to, veteran status, uniform service member status, race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, sexual orientation, genetic information, physical or mental disability, or any other protected class under federal, state, or local law. This policy governs all aspects of employment at MBLI, including hiring, assignments, training, promotions, compensation, benefits, discipline, discharge, and all other terms and conditions of employment.

You may discuss equal employment opportunity related questions with your Supervisor, Human Resources, or your School Leader.

General Harassment, Discrimination & Professional Bias

Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana (MBLI) is committed to maintaining a professional work environment grounded in respect, equity, and accountability. Consistent with MBLI’s Professional Conduct Policy, all employees are expected to interact with colleagues, students, families, and stakeholders in a manner that upholds dignity, professionalism, and mutual respect.

Violations of this policy constitute violations of the Professional Conduct Policy and may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

Harassment and Discrimination

MBLI prohibits harassment of or discrimination against any employee based on race, religion, color, national origin, physical or mental disability, marital status, military or veteran status, age, sex, sexual orientation, personal appearance, political affiliation, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law.

Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to: epithets, slurs, derogatory comments or jokes, intimidation, negative stereotyping, threats, physical interference, or any conduct that interferes with an employee’s work or creates a hostile, intimidating, or offensive work environment. Harassment or discrimination may occur in any form, including verbal, written, visual, or electronic communications such as email, text messages, online platforms, images, gestures, or postings related to the workplace.

Professional Bias

In alignment with MBLI’s expectations for professional conduct, the organization also prohibits professional bias — when an employee’s role, discipline, licensure type, degree, experience, or career pathway is unfairly elevated or diminished in ways that undermine trust, collaboration, or respect. Examples include:

  • Devaluing colleagues based on subject area, role, or credential
  • Questioning a colleague’s legitimacy or competence due to licensure type or career path
  • Implying that certain disciplines, roles, or leadership tracks are less important to student success or organizational effectiveness

All instructional, operational, and leadership roles are essential to MBLI’s mission. Professional disagreement is expected to focus on ideas, evidence, student outcomes, data, and instructional effectiveness — not personal qualifications or perceived status.

Sexual Harassment & Unwelcome Sexual Conduct

MBLI is committed to providing a workplace free of sexual harassment and other unwelcome sexual conduct — conduct that creates an offensive or hostile working environment, or that is made a condition of working at MBLI. Sexual harassment may occur between people of the same or opposite sex. Examples of prohibited conduct include:

  • Verbal harassment (e.g., sexual requests, comments, jokes, slurs)
  • Physical harassment (e.g., unwanted physical contact)
  • Visual harassment (e.g., posters, cartoons, emails, or drawings of a sexual nature)

Our workplace is not limited to MBLI’s facilities but includes anywhere a business or school-related function is taking place, including all MBLI-sponsored events. Such behavior may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Scope: This policy applies to all MBLI employees — including certified, classified, administrative, and leadership staff — and governs all professional interactions conducted in person or through written, digital, or electronic means. If an employee believes they have been subjected to conduct that violates this policy, they should report the concern promptly in accordance with the reporting procedures outlined below.

How to Report

An employee who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate these standards should immediately report the incident to Human Resources or their School Leader/supervisor. Reports may be made in person, in writing, or by telephone.

MBLI maintains an Open Door policy. Employees are never required to confront the individual involved before making a report. All reports are reviewed promptly and handled as confidentially as possible.

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith or participates in an investigation is strictly prohibited, and will be addressed as a serious violation of professional conduct.

Standards of Conduct

Employees are expected to conduct themselves in a mature and responsible manner. The following are examples of conduct that may result in disciplinary action, including suspension or termination. This list is illustrative only:

  • Conduct that diminishes confidence in your ability to perform your duties or compromises MBLI’s reputation
  • Theft or unauthorized removal or possession of property belonging to another
  • Falsification of timekeeping or other records
  • Working under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs
  • Fighting or threatening violence in the workplace
  • Boisterous or disruptive activity in the workplace
  • Negligence or improper conduct leading to damage of MBLI property
  • Insubordination or other disrespectful conduct
  • Violation of safety or health rules
  • Sexual or other unlawful harassment or discrimination
  • Possession of dangerous or unauthorized materials in the workplace
  • Excessive absenteeism or any absence without notice
  • Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information
  • Inappropriate activities involving children
  • Providing false or misleading information
  • Failure to cooperate in or interfering with an MBLI investigation

Nothing in this policy is intended to interfere with employees’ rights under the National Labor Relations Act to engage in protected concerted activity.

Conflicts of Interest Policy

MBLI is committed to maintaining the highest standards of integrity and transparency in all of its operations.

Acceptance of Gifts

Before accepting any gift from a vendor, individual, or organization with whom you or MBLI has a past, current, or potential future relationship, you must first consult with MBLI’s General Counsel or Chief Operations Officer (COO). This policy applies regardless of the value of the proposed gift or the employee’s specific role.

Service on External Boards

Any employee invited to serve on the board of directors or advisory board of another organization must disclose this in writing to both their Head of School and the COO before accepting. MBLI reserves the right to request that an employee decline or resign from such a position if it is incompatible with the employee’s duties or MBLI’s mission.

Reporting & Enforcement

Failure to disclose a potential or actual conflict of interest may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination. Employees are encouraged to seek guidance from the General Counsel or COO if unsure whether a situation may constitute a conflict.

Whistleblower Policy & Procedures

MBLI is committed to lawful and ethical behavior and requires the board, advisors, employees, and volunteers to act in accordance with all applicable laws, regulations, and policies. The objectives of the Whistleblower Policy are to prevent, detect, and correct improper activities; encourage good-faith reporting of material violations of law or policy; ensure documentation and resolution of reports; and protect individuals who report concerns in good faith, in accordance with Indiana Code § 22-5-3-3, from retaliation.

Reporting Concerns

Employees: Whenever possible, resolve concerns by reporting directly to your Supervisor or the next level of management. If those channels cannot be used, contact the Chief Operating Officer. Reports should be in writing whenever practical.

Board Members, Advisors & Volunteers: May submit concerns directly to the chair of the Board. MBLI will investigate all reports with due care and promptness.

The types of concerns that should be reported include:

  • Providing false or misleading information on MBLI’s financial documents, grant reports, tax returns, or other public documents
  • Withholding material information from MBLI’s auditors, accountants, lawyers, directors, or other representatives
  • Embezzlement, private benefit, or misappropriation of funds
  • Material violation of MBLI policy, including confidentiality, conflict of interest, ethics, and document retention
  • Discrimination based on race, gender, sexual orientation, ethnicity, and disability

No director, advisor, employee, or volunteer who in good faith reports a concern shall be threatened, discriminated against, or subject to retaliation. MBLI treats all communications under this policy confidentially, except as necessary to conduct a complete and fair investigation.

Religious Accommodations

MBLI is committed to providing reasonable accommodations, where appropriate, for employees’ religious beliefs. It is the employee’s responsibility to notify their School Leader or Supervisor of the need for an accommodation. Accommodations should not interfere with or disrupt the normal routine of our organization.

How roles, categories, and eligibility are defined.

Standard full-time schedule, paid hourly. Non-exempt and eligible for overtime; generally not paid during breaks unless scheduled.

Work less than 30 hours/week; qualify for certain benefits or benefits required by law.
Temporary
Limited or short-term duration. Exempt or non-exempt by duties; generally not benefits-eligible unless required by law.

In addition to these categories, employees are classified as exempt or non-exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

Hourly (non-exempt) employees are paid based on hours worked, must accurately record all time, and are eligible for overtime as required by law.

Salaried (exempt) employees are paid on a salary basis, are not eligible for overtime, and are expected to fulfill their responsibilities regardless of variations in work hours.

Classification is determined by job duties and business needs, not job title, and may be reviewed or adjusted to remain compliant with the law.

Immigration Reform & Control Act

MBLI hires and employs only individuals authorized to work in the United States. In compliance with the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA), all newly hired employees must complete Form I-9 as part of onboarding. Section 1 must be completed no later than the first day of work for pay, and the School will review original documentation and complete Section 2 through an E-Verify system.

Employees with time-limited work authorization are responsible for maintaining valid authorization throughout employment and must present updated documentation before expiration. All Form I-9 records are maintained securely and accessed only by authorized staff.

Employment Authorization & Visa Sponsorship

Matchbook Learning hires individuals who are legally authorized to work in the United States and who can maintain that authorization throughout employment.

As a matter of company policy, Matchbook Learning does not sponsor employment-based visas or work authorization applications (including H-1B, TN, E-3, O-1, or other petitions), and does not sponsor applications for permanent residency (“green card” sponsorship).

Applicants and employees are responsible for maintaining valid work authorization throughout their employment. Employment offers and continued employment are contingent upon the employee’s ability to provide and maintain documentation demonstrating authorization to work in the United States, as required by applicable law.

Nothing in this policy alters Matchbook Learning’s obligations under federal, state, or local law. Matchbook Learning reserves the right to modify or make exceptions to this policy at its sole discretion, consistent with applicable legal requirements and business needs.

Internal Transfer Policy

MBLI supports internal transfers between Matchbook Learning K–8 and The Match High School & Career Center to promote staff growth while maintaining instructional continuity and operational stability.

Eligibility & Approval

Employees seeking a transfer must hold required certifications or endorsements, demonstrate relevant experience and proficiency, and have a positive performance history.

A written transfer request goes to the Supervisor, with copies to the Operations Specialist and COO, including rationale, timeline, and updated credentials. An internal interview may be required. All transfers require approval from both the sending and receiving Head of School; if consensus is not reached, the COO makes the final decision.

Timing, Status & Benefits

Transfers are typically approved between academic years; mid-year transfers are generally not permitted except in urgent circumstances with COO approval.

Employees retain employment with MBLI. Salary placement may change based on role requirements, and core benefits continue though eligibility may vary if classification changes.

Compensation & Pay Practices

How and when you are paid, and related pay policies.

Pay Periods & Payroll Schedule

Most MBLI employees are paid semi-monthly on the 15th and the last day of each month. Salaried employees are generally paid over a 12-month period. Hourly employees are generally paid during the 10-month academic year based on hours worked and approved timekeeping, and are not paid during school breaks unless scheduled (except for designated paid holidays). When a payday falls on a Saturday or Sunday, employees are normally paid the following Monday; when a payday falls on a holiday, they are normally paid the next business day.

Direct Deposit or Bank-Drafted Paycheck

All full-time and part-time employees may receive pay via direct deposit or bank-drafted payroll check. Employees who elect direct deposit may view pay stubs online through Amplify Passport at https://www.amplifyhr.com/login. Direct deposit is MBLI’s preferred method.

Bank-drafted checks are mailed to the employee’s home address on file via courier and typically arrive within 2–4 business days after payday; employees are responsible for keeping their mailing address current in Amplify.

Reimbursements & Awards

All reimbursement requests must be approved by the employee’s School Leader or Supervisor, then submitted with receipts to the COO for review. Once approved by the COO, the reimbursable amount is added to the employee’s next regular paycheck.

MBLI may also recognize positive performance through monetary awards, generally issued through the next scheduled paycheck and subject to applicable payroll taxes.

Stipend Agreement Policy

MBLI may offer stipend payments for approved work outside of an employee’s regular job responsibilities, such as additional duties, extended time commitments, special projects, or supplemental assignments. All stipends must be approved in advance and documented through a written stipend agreement outlining the scope of work, duration, expectations, and payment terms.

Stipend payments are not guaranteed and are contingent upon meeting the agreement’s requirements. All approved stipends are paid through regular payroll in compliance with the FLSA and do not alter an employee’s base salary or exemption status.

Payroll Corrections & Overpayments

Employees are responsible for reviewing each paycheck for accuracy and must report suspected errors to HR or Payroll as soon as possible. If an employee is underpaid, MBLI corrects the error promptly after verification, via an off-cycle payment or the next regular paycheck. If an employee is overpaid, MBLI notifies the employee and explains repayment options; deductions comply with Indiana Code 22-2-6-2 and require written authorization. Repayment will not reduce pay below minimum wage, and reasonable repayment plans may be arranged. Employees will not be retaliated against for reporting payroll errors.

Timekeeping

Non-exempt (hourly) employees must accurately record all time worked each day using the method provided by their School Leader/Supervisor, submit timesheets by established deadlines, and record all applicable leave using the appropriate codes.

Important

No employee has authority to direct another employee to underreport time worked, omit leave taken, or submit an inaccurate timesheet. If anyone instructs you to report time inaccurately, notify your manager, Human Resources, and/or the COO immediately.

Overtime

All overtime for non-exempt employees must be approved in advance in writing by the direct Supervisor (or designee). Pre-approved overtime days include First Semester Parents in Touch Day, Winter Celebration, Second Semester Parents in Touch Day, and the End of Year Student Showcase. MBLI’s workweek runs Sunday 12:01 a.m. through Saturday 11:59 p.m. Non-exempt employees are paid overtime at 1.5× their regular rate for all hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Exempt employees are not eligible for overtime pay.

Pay Deductions

MBLI is required by federal and state law to make certain deductions each pay period, including federal income tax, state and local income tax, and Social Security and Medicare taxes (FICA). Additional deductions may include benefit premiums, retirement contributions, court-ordered garnishments, or other legally authorized withholdings. Exempt (salaried) employees’ pay will not be reduced in a manner that violates salary basis requirements; salaries may be reduced only in the following circumstances:

  • Absences of one or more full days for personal reasons other than sickness or disability
  • Absences of one or more full days due to sickness or disability, provided a bona fide sick leave or disability plan exists and the employee has exhausted all available leave under that plan
  • Unpaid leave taken under the FMLA
  • Full-day suspensions for violations of safety rules of major significance
  • Full-day disciplinary suspensions for violations of written workplace conduct policies
  • Proportional salary during the first and last weeks of employment based on days actually worked
  • Reduction for negative leave balances, applied only in full-day increments

When wages are garnished by court order, MBLI is legally bound to withhold the amount indicated, honoring federal and applicable state guidelines that protect a certain amount of income from garnishment.

Staff Attendance & Punctuality

Attendance and punctuality are essential to employee success and to consistent support for students. Employees should refer to their campus-specific handbook for detailed attendance procedures. If you will be late or absent, notify your School Leader or Supervisor before the start of the workday. Lateness is arriving after your scheduled start time; any unapproved late arrival is charged against available PTO in the nearest quarter-hour increment. If an employee is absent for three consecutive workdays without notice, MBLI will consider the position voluntarily abandoned (this does not apply when the absence may qualify for protected leave).

Qualifying military service is granted a leave of absence, generally unpaid unless otherwise required by law. Upon timely return, eligible employees are promptly reemployed in the position they would have attained (or a comparable position) with the same seniority, status, and pay. Retaliation or discrimination based on military service is strictly prohibited.

Unpaid Leave & Absenteeism

Consistent with applicable law, employees may be granted unpaid leave in appropriate circumstances; contact your School Leader, Supervisor, or HR Manager for more information. Unapproved or excessive absenteeism may negatively impact an employee’s record, evaluations, and contract status. Documented and legally protected absences will not be counted as excessive absenteeism, and MBLI will not take corrective action that interferes with an employee’s exercise of rights under applicable leave or disability laws.

Family & Medical Leave Act (FMLA)

MBLI complies with the FMLA, providing eligible employees with job-protected unpaid leave for qualifying family and medical reasons. Employees are eligible if employed by MBLI for at least 12 months (not necessarily consecutive), having worked at least 1,250 hours in the preceding 12 months, at a worksite where MBLI employs 50 or more employees within a 75-mile radius.

Eligible employees may take up to 12 workweeks of unpaid FMLA leave during a rolling 12-month period, and up to 26 workweeks of Military Caregiver Leave in a single 12-month period. Qualifying reasons include the employee’s own serious health condition; birth and bonding within 12 months; placement for adoption or foster care and bonding within 12 months; care for a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition; a qualifying exigency related to covered military service; and Military Caregiver Leave.

Notice: foreseeable leave requires at least 30 days’ advance notice when practicable; unforeseeable leave requires notice as soon as practicable. Medical certification must be submitted within 15 calendar days of request. MBLI maintains group health coverage during FMLA leave under the same terms, with employees responsible for their premium contributions. Upon timely return, employees are reinstated to the same or an equivalent position. Retaliation for requesting or taking FMLA leave is strictly prohibited.

Attendance Blackout Periods

To ensure uninterrupted instruction and continuity of operations, planned PTO or personal leave may not be taken during designated blackout periods. The Blackout Calendar is published at the beginning of each academic year and includes:

  • The first five instructional days of each school year
  • One school day before and after Fall, Thanksgiving, Winter, and Spring Breaks
  • State or district testing windows
  • Parent–teacher conference days/evenings
  • Mandatory professional development or all-staff days
  • Major school events designated by the Head of School
  • The last five instructional days of the school year

Absences not approved or legally protected during blackout periods are unpaid and coded as an Unpaid Day Off; medical documentation is required for any absence during a blackout period to be coded as paid leave. This policy does not limit rights under FMLA, ADA, military leave, or other legally protected leave; bereavement, jury duty, and subpoenaed witness leave are governed by their respective policies.

Safety, Health & Emergency

Keeping students and staff safe.

Accommodation for Disabilities (ADA)

MBLI provides equal employment opportunities to qualified individuals with disabilities in accordance with the ADA and applicable law, and also complies with the FMLA and other medical leave laws. Employees who believe they need a workplace accommodation may request one verbally or in writing by notifying their School Leader, Supervisor, or HR, and will be asked to complete an ADA Accommodation Request Form, which initiates a good-faith interactive process. Reasonable accommodations will be provided unless doing so would impose an undue hardship or significantly disrupt school operations. MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee for requesting or using a reasonable accommodation.

Pregnancy, Childbirth & Related Conditions

In accordance with the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) and the Indiana Civil Rights Law (IC 22-9-5), MBLI provides reasonable accommodations unless doing so would create an undue hardship. Accommodations may include more frequent or longer breaks; temporary modification of duties or schedules; access to seating; temporary relief from heavy lifting or strenuous tasks; time off for prenatal appointments; temporary reassignment to alternative duties; and leave related to pregnancy or childbirth when necessary. MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against any employee who requests accommodation, reports discrimination, or participates in a related investigation.

Background Checks & Safe Hiring

In accordance with Indiana law, federal law, and applicable charter authorization requirements, MBLI requires background checks for individuals who work with or have access to students. This includes all employees, interns and student teachers, volunteers, contractors and consultants, and any individual with direct contact with students — who must successfully complete a background check prior to beginning service. Checks are conducted through MBLI’s designated vendor and include state and national criminal history checks, sex offender registry checks, and any additional screenings required by the IDOE, IPS Innovation Network, or other authorizing bodies.

Mandatory Reporting — Sexual Abuse Prevention

MBLI maintains a zero-tolerance policy for sexual abuse by any employee, volunteer, contractor, board member, or third party. All MBLI employees, volunteers, and contractors are mandatory reporters under Indiana law (IC 31-33).

Any individual who has reason to believe a child has been abused or neglected must immediately report the concern directly to the Indiana Department of Child Services (DCS) and/or local law enforcement. Reporting to a supervisor or School Leader does not replace this legal obligation. Leadership may not delay, screen, or discourage reporting. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is grounds for disciplinary action.

Alcohol, Drug & Tobacco Free Workplace

MBLI is committed to maintaining a drug, alcohol, and tobacco free environment. The following is prohibited on MBLI’s property (buildings, grounds, parking lots, garages, and fields) and at off-site activities with staff or students: possession, transfer, sale, distribution, use, or solicitation of illegal drugs; possession or use of alcohol (unless specifically authorized by the School Leader); possession or use of tobacco products; reporting to work intoxicated or impaired; reporting to work with a detectable amount of any illegal drug; and abuse of prescribed drugs. MBLI maintains a policy of non-discrimination and reasonable accommodation with respect to recovering addicts and alcoholics. Violations may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

Fire & Emergency Drills

MBLI conducts safety drills throughout the school year. All employees are expected to participate fully and follow directions from school leadership and designated safety staff. MBLI practices responses to multiple emergency scenarios each year, including fire drills (typically monthly), severe weather/tornado drills (typically twice per year), and lockdown/safety drills (typically twice per year).

Emergency Reporting & Building Evacuation

Employees must notify their Supervisor or School Leader immediately when an emergency occurs — accidents or injuries, medical situations, fire or the smell of smoke, bomb threats, threats of violence, or any situation that presents an immediate safety risk. If there is immediate danger, call 911 first, then notify your Manager or School Leader as soon as it is safe to do so. If an evacuation is required, leave the building quickly and orderly, do not delay to collect personal items, and do not re-enter until cleared to do so.

No Weapons in the Workplace

MBLI prohibits the possession, carrying, use, display, or sale of weapons on MBLI property, in MBLI vehicles, at MBLI-sponsored events, or while conducting MBLI business on or off campus. “Weapons” include firearms, ammunition, knives (other than small pocketknives used for lawful purposes), tasers, explosives, and any object used or intended to cause harm. This applies to all employees, volunteers, contractors, visitors, and others on school premises, including individuals legally permitted to carry a firearm. The only exceptions are authorized law enforcement officers and individuals specifically authorized in writing by MBLI. Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, removal from campus, and referral to law enforcement.

School Closings & Delays

MBLI generally follows Indianapolis Public Schools (IPS) for weather-related and emergency closings, delays, and early dismissals across both campuses. If MBLI’s decision differs from IPS, MBLI communicates updates through school channels (calls/texts/emails and/or ParentSquare). On a one-hour delay, students receive breakfast in the cafeteria upon arrival (not in classrooms), lunch times generally remain the same, and school leadership adjusts the daily schedule to preserve core instructional minutes. Each campus publishes and follows its own delay-day schedule.

Student Safety & Mandatory Reporting (Indiana)

All MBLI employees, interns, volunteers, and contractors who work with students are considered mandatory reporters. If you have reason to believe or suspect that a student has been abused or neglected, you are required to immediately make a report to the Indiana Department of Child Services (DCS) Child Abuse and Neglect Hotline at 1-800-800-5556, or local law enforcement.

Reporting a concern to a colleague, School Leader, counselor, or social worker does not fulfill your legal obligation. After reporting to DCS or law enforcement, promptly notify internal school leadership. Employees should not attempt to investigate suspected abuse or neglect — observe, document basic facts, and report immediately. Failure to report as required by law may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, and potential legal consequences under Indiana law.

Workplace Searches & Inspections

MBLI reserves the right to conduct reasonable searches and inspections on school premises and at school-sponsored events, including items brought onto or removed from school property (packages, bags, purses, briefcases, backpacks) as well as MBLI-owned property such as desks, offices, workspaces, lockers, computers, and devices. Employees who refuse to cooperate, or who are found in possession of stolen property, illegal substances, or other prohibited items, may be subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Workplace Violence Prevention

Workplace violence of any kind will not be tolerated, whether committed by an employee, student, parent, visitor, contractor, or any other individual. Prohibited conduct includes physical violence, threats, intimidation, harassment, or other behavior that creates a reasonable fear of harm. Employees must not engage in physical or verbal confrontation with a potentially violent individual. If you encounter an immediate threat of harm, contact 911 first, then notify your School Leader or Manager as soon as it is safe to do so.

Employee Physical Requirements

Because MBLI staff may be required to move classroom materials, supplies, or equipment, employees in many roles should be able to lift, carry, push, or pull up to approximately 40 pounds, with or without reasonable accommodation. MBLI provides reasonable accommodations to qualified individuals with disabilities in accordance with the ADA and applicable law.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Use Policy

MBLI encourages the responsible use of AI tools to support instructional planning, administrative efficiency, and professional work. AI may be used as a support tool, but may not replace professional judgment, ethical decision-making, or required school processes.

Permitted: drafting lesson plans, generating ideas or outlines, summarizing information, and supporting administrative work — provided all AI-generated content is reviewed, edited, and approved by the staff member before use. Prohibited: inputting confidential or personally identifiable student or employee information; making final decisions on student discipline, grading without review, placement, special education, or employment matters; generating discriminatory, biased, misleading, or inappropriate content; and bypassing professional responsibilities or academic integrity expectations. Staff must comply with FERPA and all confidentiality requirements — when in doubt, do not input the information.

Employees managing official MBLI accounts must follow branding and communication guidelines and obtain required approvals. Nothing in this policy is intended to interfere with employee communications protected by the National Labor Relations Act.

Discipline, Complaints & Separation

Fair processes for concerns, corrective action, and departures.

Progressive Discipline Steps

Disciplinary action may include the following steps. The level of discipline applied depends on the nature, severity, frequency, and impact of the issue.

1
Verbal Warning
Addresses minor or first-time concerns. The supervisor discusses the issue, expectations, and required improvement; may include coaching. Documented internally.
2
Written Warning
Issued when concerns persist or the issue is more serious. Outlines the concern, policy reference, expectations, timeline, and consequences; placed in the personnel file.
3
PIP or Final Written Warning
Used when performance or conduct does not improve, or the issue significantly impacts students, staff, or operations. Includes defined goals, actions, and a monitoring period.
4
Suspension (With or Without Pay)
Imposed when necessary to investigate concerns or when immediate action is required. Duration and pay status depend on circumstances and law.
5
Termination of Employment
May occur when prior corrective steps fail or when misconduct is severe. Accumulation of multiple documented incidents within a rolling 12-month period may result in termination.
Immediate Discipline or Termination

MBLI may skip any or all steps based on severity and impose immediate discipline, up to and including termination, for serious misconduct — including threats to student or staff safety; abuse, neglect, or failure to report as a mandated reporter; violence, weapons, or credible threats; theft, fraud, or falsification of records; gross insubordination; harassment, discrimination, or retaliation; violation of confidentiality or data privacy laws; and serious ethical or legal violations.

Employee rights & protections: Employees may be asked to sign documentation acknowledging receipt; refusal to sign does not invalidate the action. Discipline will not be applied in a manner that interferes with rights under FMLA, ADA, military leave, jury duty, or other legally protected leave. Employees may provide written responses for inclusion in their personnel file, and retaliation for raising concerns or participating in investigations is prohibited.

Employee Complaint & Grievance Policy

This policy provides a clear, fair process to raise and resolve work-related concerns related to working conditions, schedules, supervisory or management practices, policy interpretation, workplace conduct, operational issues, and interpersonal conflicts not involving discrimination, harassment, retaliation, or sexual misconduct (which are addressed under separate policies). When appropriate, employees are encouraged to first seek informal resolution. If informal resolution is not appropriate or successful, an employee may submit a written complaint describing the concern, relevant dates, steps taken, and the desired outcome.

1 · Immediate Supervisor→ 2 · Head of School→ 3 · Human Resources / COO→ 4 · School Board

Concerns are handled with discretion and shared only with those who have a legitimate need to know. MBLI strictly prohibits retaliation against employees who raise concerns or participate in this process in good faith.

Staff Conduct Concerns & Investigation

Concerns may be reported by students, parents/guardians, employees, administrators, or community members, in writing, electronically, or verbally, and are handled discreetly on a need-to-know basis. If a formal investigation is initiated, the employee involved will be notified in writing of the allegations, their rights and responsibilities, and the expected timeline. Investigations are conducted by Operations, the Head of School, or a trained designee and may include interviews, policy review, and evidence collection; when appropriate, employees may be placed on paid administrative leave. Findings determine whether allegations are substantiated, unsubstantiated, or unfounded, and employees are notified of outcomes in writing.

Separation of Employment

Voluntary termination: Employees who resign are asked to provide advance written notice — non-certified employees at least two weeks, and certified employees who work directly with students at least four weeks during the school year, when feasible. These notice periods are requested but not required and do not alter at-will employment. Employees must return all MBLI property no later than their final day. Final pay is issued on the next regularly scheduled payroll date via direct deposit or paper check in accordance with Indiana law.

Involuntary termination: The School Leader, in coordination with Operations, determines when and how MBLI property must be returned; final pay is issued on the next regularly scheduled payroll date consistent with Indiana law. MBLI generally does not provide written letters of reference, but upon request may verify dates of employment, job title, and salary history. Departing employees are encouraged to participate in a voluntary exit interview.

COBRA & Indiana Continuation of Benefits

MBLI complies with COBRA and applicable Indiana state continuation laws, which allow eligible employees and dependents to temporarily continue group health coverage at their own expense when coverage would otherwise end due to a qualifying event (such as termination other than for gross misconduct, reduction in hours, death of the covered employee, divorce or legal separation, or a dependent child losing eligibility). Coverage duration is determined by law and may extend up to 18, 29, or 36 months; individuals electing continuation coverage are responsible for 100% of the premium plus any permitted administrative fee. Upon a qualifying event, MBLI or its administrator provides written notice explaining options, deadlines, and payment requirements.

Licensure & Professional Requirements

Credentials and training that keep us qualified.

Certification, Licensure & Highly Qualified (HQ) Status

MBLI requires all instructional and student-facing staff to hold, maintain, and remain in good standing with the appropriate state certification, licensure, or Highly Qualified (HQ) status required for their position, in accordance with Indiana Department of Education (IDOE) requirements. Employees are responsible for ensuring their licenses remain valid, active, and in good standing at all times. Employees hired without the required credential must complete an MBLI Education Plan for Certification or HQ Status; completion is a condition of continued employment in the role.

Out-of-State Licensure Requirements

Employees hired with an out-of-state teaching license are required to apply for an Indiana teaching license immediately upon hire; apply for an Indiana Emergency or Temporary Teaching Permit as applicable (to administer state-mandated assessments while the Indiana application is pending); and obtain an Indiana teaching license by the start of the following academic year, unless an extension is permitted by IDOE and approved by MBLI.

Non-compliance: Failure to maintain required licensure, comply with an approved education plan, or obtain required credentials within established timelines may result in corrective action, reassignment (if available), non-renewal of contract, or termination. Each case is reviewed individually based on documented progress and circumstances.

Professional Development & Licensure Tracking

MBLI requires all certified staff to maintain compliance with IDOE professional development and licensure renewal requirements. This applies to all instructional and student-facing employees who hold or are required to hold an Indiana teaching license, administrator license, school services license, or other IDOE-issued credential.

Employee Responsibilities
  • Complete required Professional Growth Points (PGPs) within the licensure cycle
  • Ensure PD activities meet IDOE approval criteria
  • Track and upload PGPs in the IDOE licensing system
  • Monitor license expiration dates and renewal deadlines
  • Provide PD verification to MBLI upon request
MBLI Responsibilities
  • Communicate licensure and PD expectations upon hire and annually
  • Provide or facilitate access to IDOE-aligned PD when feasible
  • Monitor licensure status and PD compliance for audit and accreditation
  • Notify employees of identified deficiencies when possible

Eligible PD may include IDOE-approved trainings, conferences, school-provided PD, applicable coursework, coaching, PLCs, and compliance trainings that qualify under IDOE guidelines. Employees must retain PD documentation for the licensure cycle; MBLI may audit records and require corrective plans if compliance gaps are identified.

CPR / AED Certification

All certified staff and any teachers or other staff whose roles require IDOE licensure/certification or an IDOE-issued permit are required to maintain current CPR and AED certification as a condition of assignment. Certification must be current and include CPR and AED (adult and child/school-age appropriate), completed through an approved provider (e.g., American Red Cross, American Heart Association, National Safety Council, or comparable), and kept valid and in good standing at all times — renewed before expiration. Failure to obtain or maintain required certification may result in corrective action and/or temporary restriction from student supervision duties until compliance is restored, up to and including non-renewal or termination.

Required Trainings

MBLI requires employees to complete mandatory training to support student safety, legal compliance, and effective school operations. Completion of assigned training is a condition of employment and/or continued assignment. Trainings are assigned by role and may include:

  • Mandated Reporter (IN IC 31-33)
  • Student safety / abuse prevention
  • FERPA / data privacy
  • Title IX / sexual misconduct awareness
  • Harassment / discrimination prevention
  • Emergency procedures
  • Technology acceptable use / data security
  • CPR / AED (when required)
  • Vector Solutions modules or other MBLI-designated platforms

Employees must complete training by the stated deadlines (and when applicable, before working with students). Failure to complete required training on time may result in corrective action, including restriction from student-facing duties, discipline, or separation, consistent with law and MBLI policy.

Workplace Expectations & Operations

Day-to-day practices and professional standards.

Corporate Credit Card Policy

MBLI corporate credit cards are issued for approved business expenses only and may not be used for personal purchases, cash advances, or cash-equivalent transactions. All corporate credit card purchases must be requested and processed through the COO; staff may not independently make purchases on an MBLI corporate card unless specifically authorized by the COO for that transaction. All charges must be supported by an itemized receipt and submitted to the COO (or designee) within ten business days of purchase. Misuse or failure to follow this process may result in loss of purchasing privileges, corrective action, and reimbursement to MBLI. Lost or stolen cards must be reported immediately to the COO and the employee’s School Leader.

Solicitation & Distribution Policy

Employees may not engage in solicitation during work time or distribute literature in work areas during work time, and may not solicit students, families, or visitors for personal business ventures, commercial products, or outside organizations while performing job duties or during instructional time. Limited exception for youth fundraisers: employees may share information or accept orders for their children’s youth-related fundraising (e.g., Girl Scout cookies, Boy Scout popcorn, youth sports) provided sales occur only during non-working time; coworkers are not pressured to participate; sales do not interfere with operations or instructional time; and materials are shared only in staff areas (not distributed to students or families without prior administrative approval).

Employee Interactions with Students

To protect employees, students, and MBLI from allegations, rumors, or lawsuits, all MBLI faculty and staff will:

  • Not allow any student(s) or alumni to spend the night at his/her house or apartment
  • Not spend time outside of regular school hours with student(s) or alumni unless approved by the School Leader
  • Not engage in any activities that would endanger or corrupt any of our student(s) or alumni
  • Always behave to protect the safety, interests, and rights of all of our students and alumni
  • Report any misconduct that endangers the safety, interests, and rights of our students and alumni
  • Not engage in any inappropriate acts or behaviors with any MBLI student(s) or alumni
  • Notify the School Leader upon noticing, suspecting, or witnessing any inappropriate behavior between students and staff members, faculty, parents, volunteers, or other MBLI-affiliated personnel

Personal Property

MBLI is not responsible for loss or damage to personal property. Valuable personal items, such as purses, wallets, personal computers, iPads, and other valuables, should not be left in areas where theft might occur.

Professional Appearance

To model professionalism for our students, employees are expected to present a neat, professional appearance at all times. Any employee appearing for work in unsuitable attire may be asked to go home and change clothes.

Substitute Personnel Usage

MBLI utilizes substitute personnel to ensure continuity of instruction and maintain safe operations. Substitute teachers may provide temporary coverage for certified instructional positions and must meet applicable IDOE requirements, assigned only to roles for which they are properly credentialed. Substitute teachers shall not be used to fill classified positions, except in limited, documented emergency situations with prior approval from the Head of School or COO. All substitute assignments must comply with IDOE licensure requirements, contracted vendor requirements, and MBLI policies.
