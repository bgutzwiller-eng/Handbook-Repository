Hiring & Exiting Staff Playbook

2026
Purpose

Consistent, compliant procedures across the full employee lifecycle.

This Playbook establishes clear, consistent, and legally compliant procedures for recruiting, hiring, supporting, and separating employees across Matchbook Learning Schools of Indiana (MBLI). It covers the full employee lifecycle — from workforce planning and recruitment through onboarding, performance management, and separation — and serves as the operational guide for school leaders, Human Resources staff, and executive leadership.

All staffing decisions must reflect the highest standards of integrity, transparency, accountability, and student-centered leadership. This Playbook supplements, and does not replace, the Employee Handbook.

Purpose & Guiding Principles

Why this Playbook exists, and who it applies to.

Guiding Principles

PrincipleWhat It Means
Student-CenteredStudent safety, well-being, and academic continuity are the primary consideration in every staffing decision.
Integrity & FairnessAll employees are treated with respect and professionalism. Decisions are based on objective criteria and documented evidence.
ConsistencyUniform procedures reduce confusion and ensure equitable treatment across schools and departments.
Legal ComplianceAll employment actions comply with applicable federal, state, and local law. HR is consulted before high-risk decisions are finalized.
DocumentationAll significant employment decisions — hiring, discipline, investigations, and separations — are properly documented.
Dignity in TransitionsSeparations, particularly involuntary ones, are handled with professionalism and respect for all individuals.

Scope

This Playbook applies to all MBLI employees and leadership roles — teachers, administrators, instructional support, operations, and network-level staff — across the full employment lifecycle. It governs workforce planning, recruitment, hiring, onboarding, performance management, investigations, mandatory reporting, removal from duty, separations, and offboarding.

Situations involving student safety, legal compliance, or employee misconduct require consultation with HR, executive leadership, or legal counsel before final decisions are made. MBLI reserves the right to update procedures as needed to reflect changes in law, policy, or operational needs.

Decision Authority & Leadership Roles

Who decides what, and when HR must be involved.

Overview

Clear lines of authority protect students, ensure legal compliance, and prevent inconsistent employment actions. The table below summarizes decision-making responsibility across leadership levels.

RolePrimary ResponsibilitiesRequires HR / ELT Approval For
Head of SchoolIdentifies needs, leads interviews, recommends hires, manages performance, initiates discipline, manages transitionsFinal terminations, investigations involving serious allegations, deviations from hiring procedures
Human ResourcesPolicy compliance, candidate screening, licensure verification, disciplinary guidance, investigations, personnel recordsMust review all: new hires, terminations, suspensions, layoffs, misconduct investigations, legally sensitive decisions
Executive Leadership (COO)Approves staffing plans and structures, ensures budget alignment, oversees high-risk decisionsPosition creation/elimination, large-scale restructuring, reductions in force, significant legal risk
Leadership Team (APs, Coaches)Participates in interviews, observes performance, provides coaching, reports safety concernsNo independent authority to hire, discipline, or terminate

Mandatory Reporting — All Staff

All MBLI employees are mandated reporters under Indiana law and must report suspected child abuse or neglect immediately to the Indiana DCS Hotline (1-800-800-5556) or local law enforcement. School leadership must also be notified that a report was made. Failure to report is a violation of state law and may result in disciplinary action.

See Appendix B: DCS Reporting Decision Tree

Escalation — When to Involve HR Immediately

School leaders must contact HR without delay when:

  • A staff allegation involves student safety or misconduct
  • Termination or non-renewal is being considered
  • A legal or compliance concern arises
  • An employee raises discrimination, harassment, or retaliation concerns
  • An employee is placed on administrative leave or suspended
  • An employee is arrested or criminally charged (see Appendix G)

Decision-Making Checklist

Before finalizing any significant staffing action, confirm:

  • Does this decision prioritize student safety and learning continuity?
  • Is the decision consistent with policy and past practice?
  • Is there clear, objective documentation supporting the decision?
  • Has HR been consulted where required?
  • Does the decision comply with applicable law and reporting obligations?
03

Workforce Planning

The annual staffing planning process, step by step.

Annual Staffing Planning Process

Each year, school leaders and executive leadership collaborate to develop a staffing plan that ensures appropriate coverage, instructional quality, and fiscal sustainability. The plan must be completed prior to the start of recruitment for the following school year.

1
Enrollment Projections
Review current enrollment trends, retention data, expected new enrollment, grade-level cohort sizes, and any program expansions or reductions.
2
Budget Alignment
Staffing plans must fit within approved personnel budget allocations. Leadership reviews salary and benefit projections, funding sources for specialized roles (e.g., grant-funded positions), and the financial sustainability of proposed structures. Personnel costs must remain within approved budget parameters.
3
Programmatic Needs
Evaluate instructional and operational needs including intervention and student support roles, special education, multilingual learner services, counseling, electives, and instructional coaching.
4
Licensure & Compliance Review
All instructional roles must be filled by individuals who meet Indiana licensure and certification requirements. Proper licensure protects accreditation and compliance status.
5
Role Restructuring (If Needed)
Based on enrollment, budget, or program changes, leadership may need to create new positions, consolidate roles, adjust grade-level structures, or reassign responsibilities. Any significant restructuring requires HR and COO review before implementation.
04

Recruitment & Hiring

From position authorization through the employment offer.

Position Authorization

No position may be posted or filled until formally authorized. The following must be confirmed before recruitment begins:

  • Position is included in the approved staffing plan and personnel budget
  • Job description has been reviewed and approved
  • HR has verified classification and compensation structure
  • COO approval obtained if required

No recruitment activities may begin until position authorization is complete.

Job Description Standards

All positions must have a current, approved job description before recruitment begins. Descriptions must include: position title, role purpose, key responsibilities, required and preferred qualifications, licensure or certification requirements, reporting structure, physical requirements (if applicable), salary range or pay band, and work schedule. For instructional roles, licensure requirements must be explicitly stated (e.g., “Indiana Teaching License Required”). If licensure may be obtained post-hire, postings must clearly outline degree requirements and licensure pathways.

Recruitment Strategy

Recruitment efforts should attract a diverse pool of highly qualified candidates aligned with MBLI’s mission. Channels may include:

  • Education-specific job boards and state educator job banks
  • Teacher preparation programs and university partnerships
  • Employee referral programs
  • Professional networks and community partnerships
  • Online recruitment platforms

Recruitment messaging should emphasize MBLI’s educational mission, commitment to student achievement, professional integrity, and growth mindset.

Candidate Screening

Initial screening evaluates whether applicants meet minimum qualifications. Reviewers assess educational background (bachelor’s degree minimum for certified positions), content alignment, professional experience, licensure status, and evidence of instructional effectiveness for teaching roles.

Candidates without current licensure may only advance if they (a) meet degree requirements AND (b) are eligible for an emergency permit or enrolled in a licensure program.

Interview Process

Interview Stages

StageFocus
Stage 1 — ScreeningConducted by school leadership or HR. Qualifications, professional background, and general fit.
Stage 2 — Competency InterviewPanel or structured interview. Instructional knowledge, problem-solving, leadership. Teaching candidates may deliver a demonstration lesson.
Stage 3 — Leadership Review (if applicable)Final interview with executive leadership or school board. Alignment with mission and expectations.

Interview Standards

  • All interviews use structured questions and a consistent evaluation rubric
  • Interviewers document responses and provide feedback before hiring decisions are made
  • Licensure status is a formal decision factor for all instructional, administrator, and director roles

Reference Checks

At least two professional references should be verified before an offer is finalized, including at minimum a current or former supervisor. Reference questions may address professional reliability, instructional effectiveness, collaboration, professional conduct, and areas for development. All reference documentation is maintained in the candidate’s hiring record.

Background Checks & Compliance Verification

All candidates must successfully complete the following prior to employment: criminal background check, child abuse registry check, and verification of professional licensure.

Important

Employment offers are contingent upon successful completion of all required checks. Candidates may not begin work until clearance is confirmed.

Employment Offer Process

Once a candidate is selected and all pre-employment checks are cleared, HR processes the formal offer. The sequence is: hiring recommendation submitted to HR → HR verifies documentation and compliance → verbal offer extended → written offer letter issued → candidate signs employment agreement.

Offer letters must include: position title, compensation structure, start date, employment status, conditions of employment, benefits eligibility, and an at-will employment statement. Offers are contingent upon verification of active licensure. For non-licensed candidates, offers are contingent on an emergency permit or enrollment in an approved licensure pathway.

Hiring Documentation

HR maintains a complete hiring record for every position filled. Required documentation includes: job posting, candidate applications, interview notes and evaluation forms, reference check documentation, background check results, signed offer letter or employment agreement, and license verification for certified positions.

Equitable Hiring

All hiring decisions are based on professional qualifications, demonstrated competencies, and mission alignment. All interviewers are expected to conduct evaluations in a professional, objective, and respectful manner. MBLI does not discriminate on any basis protected by federal, state, or local law.

05

Onboarding

Pre-employment documentation through the first 90 days.

Pre-Employment Documentation

Before the employee’s first day, HR and school leadership ensure all required documentation is completed:

  • Employment eligibility verification (I-9)
  • Federal and state tax forms (W-4)
  • Direct deposit authorization
  • Background check authorization (if not already completed)
  • Employee Handbook acknowledgment
  • Emergency contact and employee information form
  • Benefits enrollment forms (if applicable)
  • Professional certification is valid (when applicable)

For instructional roles, HR must verify valid teaching license, subject-area endorsements, and compliance with state and authorizer requirements. If licensure is conditional or pending, HR monitors timelines and notifies leadership of any compliance risk.

First-Day Orientation

All new employees participate in orientation — at the network level, school level, or both — covering:

  • MBLI mission, values, and educational model
  • Introduction to the leadership team
  • Student safety procedures and mandatory reporting obligations
  • School policies, professional expectations, and staff culture
  • Technology systems, communication tools, and building procedures

School-Level Integration

School leaders ensure new staff are integrated into their teams by coordinating: introduction to grade-level or department teams, classroom setup and instructional preparation, curriculum resource access, school schedule and routine review, student support procedures, and technology platform access.

Role-Specific Training

Employees receive targeted training relevant to their responsibilities, including curriculum and instructional training (teachers), student data and assessment systems, behavior management procedures, special education compliance, technology platforms, and operational procedures (administrative staff).

First 90 Days

The first 90 days are critical. School leaders are required to:

  • Clearly communicate role expectations from day one
  • Conduct regular observations for instructional staff
  • Provide consistent coaching feedback
  • Schedule at least one formal check-in between days 60–90 to discuss role adjustment, professional goals, support needs, and alignment with expectations

Mentorship

First-year teachers and staff new to the profession should be paired with experienced colleagues who provide coaching, lesson planning support, classroom management guidance, and encouragement throughout the onboarding period. Intentional mentorship improves retention and accelerates professional growth.

Onboarding Documentation

HR and school leadership maintain records confirming completion of: all required employment forms, orientation attendance, training completions, and licensure verification. Proper documentation ensures compliance and creates a complete onboarding record.

06

Employment Management

Performance expectations, feedback, and progressive discipline.

Performance Expectations

All employees are expected to fulfill the essential duties of their role, maintain professional conduct, demonstrate reliability and punctuality, collaborate effectively, and prioritize student safety and success. Instructional staff are additionally expected to deliver standards-aligned instruction, monitor and respond to student data, maintain effective classroom management, and communicate with families. Leaders must communicate expectations clearly and enforce them consistently.

Performance Management Framework

Observation & Feedback

Leaders regularly observe employee performance. For instructional staff: formal and informal classroom observations, instructional coaching, and data review meetings. For non-instructional staff: task performance reviews, operational effectiveness, and team collaboration.

Goal Setting

Employees set professional goals aligned to student achievement, instructional improvement, and organizational priorities. Leaders review progress throughout the year.

Ongoing Communication

Performance management includes regular check-ins, coaching conversations, and mid-year and end-of-year evaluations. Consistent communication prevents misunderstanding and supports continuous improvement.

Addressing Performance Concerns

When concerns arise, leaders must address them promptly and specifically: identify the concern with documented examples, clarify expectations going forward, and offer concrete support or resources. Early intervention prevents escalation and gives employees the best opportunity to improve.

Progressive Discipline

MBLI uses a progressive discipline approach for performance and conduct concerns. MBLI reserves the right to skip steps based on severity.

StepDescription
Coaching ConversationInformal discussion focused on improvement. May or may not be documented based on severity.
Verbal WarningClearly communicated concern; documented internally by the supervisor.
Written WarningFormal documentation of the issue, expectations for improvement, and timeline for resolution.
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)Detailed plan with specific areas of concern, measurable goals, timeline, support provided, and consequences for non-compliance.
Termination RecommendationInitiated when improvement is not achieved or in cases of serious misconduct. Requires HR review and COO approval.
Important

HR must be consulted before issuing any written warning, PIP, suspension, or termination recommendation. Serious misconduct may result in immediate escalation to termination.

Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)

A PIP is used when an employee requires structured support to meet expectations. Every PIP must include: specific areas of concern, clear and measurable performance expectations, timeline for improvement, support and resources to be provided, and consequences if improvement is not achieved. Leaders meet regularly with the employee during the PIP period to monitor progress and provide documented feedback.

Professional Conduct

All employees must maintain professional conduct at all times, including: appropriate interactions with students and colleagues, adherence to school policies and procedures, ethical decision-making, compliance with mandated reporting obligations, and maintenance of professional boundaries with students. Violations may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Attendance & Reliability

Employees must report to work on time as scheduled, follow leave request procedures, communicate absences appropriately, and ensure continuity of responsibilities when absent. Excessive absenteeism or failure to follow procedures may result in disciplinary action.

Documentation Standards

Leaders must maintain accurate, timely, objective, and fact-specific documentation of performance and conduct. This includes observation notes, feedback records, emails or written communications, disciplinary actions, and PIPs. All documentation must be stored in accordance with HR guidelines. Documentation protects employees and the organization and supports fair, defensible employment decisions.

Staff Separation & Offboarding

Every separation type, and the offboarding timeline.

Types of Separation

CategoryTypes
VoluntaryResignation, retirement
InvoluntaryNon-renewal of contract, termination for performance, termination for misconduct, reduction in force (layoff)
OtherJob abandonment, contract expiration

Leaders must correctly identify the separation type before initiating procedures. Each type has distinct documentation, notification, and legal requirements.

Resignation

Employee Responsibilities

  • Submit a written resignation notice
  • Provide appropriate notice (typically two weeks, or per contract terms)
  • Support transition planning when possible

Leadership Responsibilities

  • Acknowledge receipt of resignation in writing
  • Notify HR immediately
  • Determine and confirm final working date
  • Develop a transition plan to maintain student continuity
  • Communicate appropriately with relevant stakeholders

Required Documentation

  • Signed resignation letter
  • Written acknowledgment from leadership
  • Final employment date confirmation

Non-Renewal of Contract

Non-renewal occurs when a contract is not extended for the following year based on performance concerns, programmatic changes, or organizational needs.

Process: Review performance documentation → Consult HR → Confirm decision with executive leadership if required → Provide written notice within required timelines → Conduct a professional notification meeting.

Non-renewal notices must be issued in writing within the timelines required by Indiana law, the employee’s contract, and MBLI policy. Consult HR for specific deadlines.

Termination for Performance

Termination for performance occurs when an employee has not met expectations despite documented support and intervention.

Requirements: Clear documentation of performance concerns and feedback provided; evidence that progressive discipline steps were followed; HR review prior to final decision; COO approval.

Process: Review documentation and performance history → Consult HR → Confirm decision and legal risk assessment (see Appendix K) → Conduct termination meeting → Issue written documentation.

Termination for Misconduct

Termination for misconduct may occur when an employee engages in conduct that violates policy, professional standards, or student safety requirements. Examples include inappropriate conduct with students, policy violations, ethical breaches, safety violations, or criminal behavior.

Important

Serious misconduct may result in immediate termination without progressive discipline.

Process: Conduct investigation in coordination with HR → Review evidence and findings → Determine appropriate action → Obtain HR and COO approval → Conduct termination meeting.

See Appendix A: Investigation Protocols | Appendix C: Immediate Removal from Classroom | Appendix E: Student Allegation Interview Protocols | Appendix K: Legal Risk Checkpoints for Termination

Reduction in Force (Layoff)

Layoffs may occur due to enrollment decline, budget constraints, or programmatic changes. Decisions must be based on role criteria, not individual characteristics.

Process: Identify affected positions → Review budget and staffing model → Consult HR and executive leadership → Develop communication plan (see Appendix D) → Notify impacted employees individually in writing.

All layoff decisions must clearly communicate that the action is organization- or program-driven, not performance-based (unless performance is a contributing factor). Consistency and fairness across the selection process are non-negotiable.

Termination / Exit Meeting Protocol

All involuntary separations require a structured meeting. The meeting must be conducted privately, with HR present when appropriate, and must be clear and direct. Avoid unnecessary debate or disclosure of confidential details.

Topics to address: Final employment status, effective date of separation, final pay and benefits information (direct to HR), return of property, and next steps.

Exit Interviews

Exit interviews may be conducted for voluntary separations. Participation is voluntary but encouraged. The purpose is to identify trends in staff experience, improve retention strategies, and gather candid feedback on leadership and culture. HR maintains all exit interview records confidentially.

Offboarding Procedures

Timeline

WhenRequired Actions
Day 0 — Decision MadeNotify HR. Determine separation type and effective date. Develop communication and transition plan. Initiate access removal plan for involuntary separations.
Final Day (or Immediately for Involuntary)Conduct exit meeting. Collect all physical property. Disable all system and building access. Confirm final handoff of responsibilities.
1–3 Business Days AfterProcess final payroll and benefits. Update personnel records. Confirm all assets returned. Finalize all separation documentation.

Asset & Property Recovery

All of the following must be collected on or before the final day: keys and building access cards, ID badges, laptops, tablets, and other devices, school-issued equipment, and instructional materials or curriculum resources. A checklist must be used to confirm all items are accounted for.

Systems & Access Removal

Access must be removed from all systems immediately for involuntary separations and by end-of-day for voluntary separations (unless otherwise directed). Systems include: email accounts, student information systems (PowerSchool), learning management systems, internal HR platforms, shared drives, and building access systems.

Final Pay & Benefits

HR processes all compensation in accordance with applicable law and MBLI policy: regular wages through the final workday, any accrued leave payout (if applicable), and any additional compensation owed. Employees are directed to HR for all questions about benefits termination, COBRA continuation options, and retirement accounts.

Transition Planning

Leadership must ensure continuity of student instruction and operations by: reassigning responsibilities promptly, securing substitute coverage if needed, supporting student transitions, and communicating with staff appropriately. For instructional roles, lesson plans, curriculum resources, and active student case notes must be transferred before or on the final day.

Communication Protocol

Leaders communicate staff changes carefully and professionally. Confidential details may not be shared. Speculation and informal discussion are prohibited. Communications should be concise and respectful.

Recommended staff communication: “This staff member is no longer with Matchbook Learning. We appreciate their contributions and are focused on supporting our students and staff moving forward.”

Separation Documentation

For every separation, maintain: resignation or termination records, performance documentation, investigation findings (if applicable), communication records, final employment status confirmation, and all offboarding checklists. All records are stored in accordance with HR retention policies.

HR must be involved in all involuntary separations, any situation involving potential legal risk, layoffs, and cases involving misconduct or investigation.

08

Record Keeping

Personnel file requirements, confidentiality, and retention.

Personnel File Requirements

HR maintains official personnel files for all employees. Files must contain: employment application and offer letter, I-9 verification (stored separately per law), tax and payroll forms, job description, signed handbook acknowledgment, performance evaluations and observation records, disciplinary documentation, training and licensure verification, and separation documentation.

Documentation Standards

All employment-related documentation must be:

  • Objective and fact-based — free of personal opinions or emotional language
  • Specific and detailed — describes who, what, when, where with enough detail to stand alone if reviewed later
  • Timely — completed as soon as practicable after the event
  • Professional in tone — appropriate for review by HR, legal counsel, or external parties
  • Stored securely in approved MBLI systems only — personal devices, personal email, and unsecured folders are prohibited

Confidentiality & Access

Personnel records are strictly confidential. Access is limited to individuals with a legitimate need to know (HR, the employee’s direct supervisor, and authorized leadership). Supervisors may not store disciplinary or personnel records in personal files, email inboxes, or unsecured locations. Employees may access their own records as permitted by Indiana law and MBLI policy — requests must be directed to HR.

Records Retention

MBLI retains personnel records in accordance with applicable federal and Indiana law. At minimum:

  • General personnel records: retained for a minimum of 3 years after separation
  • I-9 forms: retained for 3 years from hire date or 1 year after separation, whichever is later
  • Payroll records: retained for a minimum of 3 years
  • Records related to investigations or legal matters: retained as directed by legal counsel

HR is responsible for establishing and communicating the current records retention schedule. Leaders must not destroy employment records without HR authorization.

Prohibited Practices

The following are strictly prohibited:

  • Maintaining unofficial or “shadow” personnel files
  • Storing disciplinary records in personal email or on personal devices
  • Sharing confidential personnel information without authorization
  • Retroactively altering or back-dating employment records
  • Destroying records without HR authorization
Important

Intentional falsification or unauthorized destruction of records may result in termination and referral to appropriate authorities.

The decision authority matrix and required collaboration points.

Shared Responsibilities

Effective employment management requires ongoing collaboration between school leadership and HR. Neither operates in isolation — personnel decisions must be coordinated to ensure consistency, compliance, and protection of students and staff.

Required Collaboration Points

School leaders must partner with HR at each of the following milestones:

  • Before initiating any formal disciplinary action (written warning or higher)
  • Before placing an employee on administrative leave or suspension
  • Before initiating non-renewal or termination proceedings
  • When student safety or mandatory reporting obligations are involved
  • When an employee raises a discrimination, harassment, or retaliation concern
  • When an arrest, criminal charge, or law enforcement contact is reported (Appendix G)
  • Before communicating a RIF or restructuring decision to affected staff

Communication Expectations

All employment-related communications — verbal, written, and digital — must be professional, factual, confidential, and appropriately routed. Leaders may not communicate disciplinary decisions, separation actions, or investigation findings to staff, students, or families without HR authorization. Sensitive matters may not be communicated through personal email, text, or informal channels.

Accountability & Compliance

Failure to follow the protocols in this Playbook — including bypassing HR review, maintaining shadow files, issuing unauthorized discipline, or failing to report mandatory reporting obligations — constitutes a leadership performance issue and may result in corrective action. HR monitors compliance across both campuses and provides guidance proactively.

Ethics & Culture

Standards, equity, confidentiality, and ethical decision-making.

Ethical Practice Standards

All MBLI leaders and staff are expected to act with integrity, honesty, and transparency in all employment matters. Ethical conduct includes: making decisions based on objective evidence and organizational need, avoiding conflicts of interest, maintaining professional boundaries, treating all individuals with dignity, and upholding MBLI’s mission in every interaction.

Student-Centered Culture

Every employment decision — from hiring to separation — must be evaluated through the lens of its impact on students. Staffing choices that protect instructional continuity, safety, and student relationships are always prioritized over administrative convenience. Leaders are accountable for ensuring that classroom and school culture remain stable through personnel transitions.

Equity & Fairness

MBLI is committed to equitable employment practices at every stage of the employee lifecycle. This includes ensuring that hiring, evaluation, discipline, and separation decisions are free from bias and applied consistently across roles, campuses, and individuals. Leaders must be alert to patterns that suggest disparate treatment and raise concerns with HR immediately.

Confidentiality

All personnel matters are strictly confidential. Leaders may not share information about employee performance, disciplinary actions, investigations, or separations with staff, students, families, or community members, except as authorized by HR or required by law. Violations of confidentiality obligations are a serious professional conduct issue and will be addressed accordingly.

Reporting Ethical Concerns

Employees and leaders are expected and encouraged to report ethical concerns, including suspected policy violations, misconduct, conflicts of interest, or retaliation. Reports may be made to HR, a supervisor, or the COO. MBLI prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.

See Employee Handbook: Whistleblower Policy for full reporting procedures and protections.

Leadership’s Role in Culture

Leaders set the tone for professional culture through their own conduct. Leaders are expected to model the values they enforce, make decisions transparently and fairly, acknowledge mistakes and course-correct, hold themselves to the same professional standards as the staff they supervise, and create an environment where concerns can be raised safely and respectfully.

Ethical Decision-Making Checklist

Before finalizing any significant employment decision, leaders should confirm:

  • Is this decision grounded in student safety and organizational mission?
  • Is it consistent with past practice and applied equitably?
  • Is it supported by objective documentation?
  • Has HR been consulted where required?
  • Would I be comfortable if this decision were reviewed externally?
